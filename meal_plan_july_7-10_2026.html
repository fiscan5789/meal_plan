Your requests, mapped

Eggs: Tuesday's frittata uses 8 large eggs as the centerpiece — fully baked until set (no runny yolk), which is the pregnancy-safe way to eat eggs. 24 oz salmon (four 6-oz fillets): Wednesday's sheet-pan salmon, roasted through with its own broccoli side. Friday's curried chicken rounds out the three nights with a different protein and cuisine.

Pregnancy-safe

All three follow the usual standard. The frittata bakes until the eggs are fully set (the recipe's test: the eggs shouldn't jiggle when you shake the pan) — no runny yolks. The salmon is roasted to 145°F fully through (a change from the recipe's "slightly rare center"). The chicken cooks to 165°F. The bacon in the frittata is fully cooked in. No alcohol; cheeses are cooked. Spinach and chicken bring iron; spinach and eggs bring folate; salmon brings DHA.

Two small pregnancy notes

Goat cheese (Tue): fresh goat cheese is fine in pregnancy as long as it's pasteurized — most US supermarket chevre is, but check the label. It's also baked here, which adds a margin of safety. Eggs (Tue): the whole point of a frittata is fully-set eggs, so this is naturally safe — just bake until there's no jiggle.

Tuesday · the egg night

Loaded Baked Frittata

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Genevieve Ko.
45 mintotal
20 minactive
Oven 375°Foven-safe skillet
4–6 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 8 largeeggs (your eggs)
  • ½ cupwhole milk
  • ½ cupfinely diced uncooked bacon
  • 1 cupdiced onion (1 small onion)
  • 2 cupsdiced red or orange bell pepper (2 peppers)
  • 1 (5-oz) pkgbaby spinach
  • 4 ozfresh goat cheese (pasteurized — check label)
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • to servegreen salad or crusty bread (optional)

Method

Heat the oven to 375°F. In a medium bowl, beat the eggs, milk, 1 tsp salt, and ½ tsp pepper until smooth. Set aside.
In a 10–12" cast-iron or oven-safe nonstick skillet, cook the bacon over medium heat, stirring, until browned, 4–5 min.
Add the onion, peppers, and ½ tsp each salt and pepper. Cook, stirring often, until the onions are translucent, 6–7 min. Add the spinach a handful at a time, stirring after each addition, until wilted.
Reduce the heat to low and pour in the egg mixture. Stir well to distribute the vegetables, then smooth the top. Drop small nuggets of goat cheese evenly over the top. Transfer to the oven.
Bake until the top is golden brown and the eggs are fully set, 20–25 min. Pregnancy: when you shake the pan, the eggs shouldn't jiggle at all — that's your signal the eggs are fully cooked and safe. Cool on a rack about 10 min.
Cut into wedges and serve warm or at room temperature, with a salad or bread if you like.
Pregnancy A frittata is one of the safest ways to eat eggs in pregnancy — the eggs cook fully through, no runny yolk. Bake until there's no jiggle. Eggs are rich in choline (key for fetal brain development) and the spinach adds iron and folate. Goat cheese: use pasteurized (standard for US chevre; check the label), and here it's baked, which is extra-safe. Bacon is fully cooked into the dish.
Make ahead / leftovers Dice the onion and peppers the night before. The frittata keeps up to 3 days in the fridge and is good cold or at room temperature — great for lunch the next day, or slice it into a sandwich (trim to fit your bread).
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Frittata is excellent finger food for both kids — soft, protein-rich, easy to hold. Cut into small strips or cubes for the 13-mo. The flavors are mild (no spice). If your kids are iffy on visible spinach, the egg holds it together so it's less noticeable. Goat cheese adds a mild tang most toddlers accept; if not, the frittata works fine without it in a kid portion.
Wednesday · the salmon night

Sheet-Pan Salmon & Broccoli
with Sesame & Ginger

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Lidey Heuck.
20 mintotal
15 minactive
Oven 425°Fone sheet pan
4 servingsuses your 24 oz salmon

Ingredients

  • 4 Tbsptoasted sesame oil, divided (pantry)
  • 2 Tbspsoy sauce or tamari (pantry)
  • 1 Tbsprice vinegar (pantry)
  • 1 Tbsphoney
  • 2 Tbspgrated fresh ginger (2" piece)
  • 1 clovegarlic, grated
  • 1 lbbroccoli, cut into florets
  • 2scallions, cut into 1½" segments, plus thinly sliced for garnish
  • 1 Tbspolive oil, plus more for brushing (pantry)
  • 4 (6 oz)skin-on salmon fillets (your 24 oz salmon)
  • ½lime, for serving
  • —sesame seeds, for serving
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • to servesteamed rice (pantry)

Method

Heat the oven to 425°F. In a small bowl, whisk 3 Tbsp of the sesame oil with the soy sauce, rice vinegar, honey, ginger, and garlic until smooth. Set the glaze aside.
Place the broccoli florets and 1½" scallion segments on a sheet pan. Drizzle with the olive oil and the remaining 1 Tbsp sesame oil, sprinkle with ½ tsp salt and ¼ tsp pepper, and toss well. Roast 5 min.
While the broccoli roasts, pat the salmon dry, brush all over with olive oil, and season with salt and pepper.
Toss the broccoli and push it to the edges of the pan, clearing space in the center. Place the salmon fillets evenly spaced in the center. Brush the fillets generously with the glaze.
Return the pan to the oven and roast until the salmon is fully cooked through — opaque and flaking easily, 145°F at the thickest part, about 12–14 min. (This is a bit longer than the recipe's "slightly rare center" — for pregnancy, cook it all the way through.)
Squeeze the lime over the broccoli and sprinkle with salt. Scatter sliced scallions and sesame seeds over the salmon. Serve hot with rice.
Pregnancy Salmon is a top pregnancy fish — low-mercury, high in DHA omega-3s. The single change: roast to 145°F (fully opaque) rather than the medium-rare center the recipe targets. A couple of extra minutes does it. Everything else is pregnancy-safe as written.
Make ahead (Tue evening, ~5 min) Whisk the sesame-ginger glaze and refrigerate. Cut the broccoli florets and scallions. Wednesday is then a 20-min roast — the fastest night of the three.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Salmon and broccoli are both great for kids. The glaze has soy sauce (salty) and honey — note honey is fine for both kids here since they're both over 12 months (never under 1). For the 13-mo, flake the salmon and check carefully for pin bones; chop the broccoli small. Brush their salmon with less glaze, or none, if you want to keep the sodium down.
Friday · South Asian-inspired

Curried Chicken & Rice
with Cranberries

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights by Christopher Kimball (2018).
45 mintotal
30 minactive
Stovetopskillet + saucepan
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 3 Tbspgrapeseed or other neutral oil, divided
  • 2 Tbspfinely grated fresh ginger
  • 1 Tbspcurry powder
  • ½ tspground cardamom
  • 1½ lbboneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1" pieces
  • 1½ cupsbasmati rice, rinsed & drained
  • 2½ cupscoconut water, divided
  • 4 largeshallots, halved & thinly sliced
  • ½ cupchopped dried cranberries
  • 2 Tbsplime juice
  • 2½ cupsfresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • to servemango chutney (optional)

Method

In a medium bowl, mix 1 Tbsp of the oil, the ginger, curry powder, cardamom, 2½ tsp salt, and 1 tsp pepper into a paste. Transfer 1 Tbsp of the paste to a large saucepan and set aside. Add the chicken to the bowl with the rest of the paste; stir to coat. Let marinate at room temperature while you cook the rice.
Stir the rinsed rice and 1¾ cups of the coconut water into the paste in the saucepan. Bring to a simmer over medium-high. Cover, reduce to low, and cook until mostly tender but still slightly firm, about 10 min. Off heat, uncover and fluff with a fork. Set aside.
In a 12" skillet over medium-high, heat the remaining 2 Tbsp oil until shimmering. Add the shallots and cook, stirring, until golden brown, about 5 min. Push them to the side; add the chicken in an even layer.
Cook the chicken without stirring until golden on the bottom, about 3 min. Stir the shallots into the chicken, add ¼ cup coconut water, and scrape up the browned bits. Cook, stirring, until the liquid evaporates, about 20 seconds. Pregnancy: make sure the chicken is cooked through to 165°F, no pink.
Add the rice, the remaining ½ cup coconut water, and the cranberries. Stir, then cook undisturbed over medium-high until the rice begins to crisp on the bottom, about 3 min.
Off heat, add the lime juice. Stir to combine, scraping up the crust. Stir in 2 cups of the cilantro; taste and season. Transfer to a platter and top with the remaining ½ cup cilantro. Serve with mango chutney if you like.
Pregnancy Chicken thighs cooked fully to 165°F — a good iron source from the dark meat. Curry powder and cardamom are spices, not concerns. No alcohol. A warming, aromatic one-dish meal that reheats well if the pregnant adult wants leftovers for lunch.
Make ahead (Thu evening, ~10 min) Slice the shallots; grate the ginger; mix the curry paste. Cut the chicken and marinate it overnight in the fridge (it only benefits). Rinse the rice. Friday is then a smooth 30-min cook.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Mild curry, usually a hit. For the 13-mo: chop the chicken small; the rice is soft and easy. Chop the dried cranberries small (whole dried fruit is a choking risk under 4) — or pull a portion before adding them. The dish isn't spicy-hot, but taste first; a spoon of plain yogurt cools it if needed. For the 34-mo, the sweet cranberries usually win them over.

Pregnancy food-safety, the working rules

  • Eggs: fully cooked, no runny yolk — the frittata bakes until set (no jiggle).
  • Fish: salmon to 145°F (opaque, flakes easily); low-mercury.
  • Poultry: chicken to 165°F, no pink.
  • Cheese: goat cheese fine if pasteurized (check label), and it's baked here.
  • Bacon: fully cooked into the frittata.
  • No alcohol in any of these dishes.

General guidance, not medical advice — defer to your OB/midwife, especially on fish frequency.

  • Basmati (Fri): any long-grain white rice.
  • Broccoli (Wed): broccolini, green beans, or asparagus roast similarly.

Monday · Italian-American

Shrimp Scampi with Orzo

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Ali Slagle. White wine swapped for stock for pregnancy safety.
25 mintotal
20 minactive
Stovetopone skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 1 lblarge shrimp, peeled & deveined
  • 3 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil, divided (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspgrated lemon zest
  • ½ tspred pepper flakes (pantry)
  • 4 clovesgarlic, minced (divided)
  • 2 Tbspunsalted butter
  • 1 cuporzo
  • ⅓ cupchicken or seafood stock (swapped for the wine)
  • 2 cupsboiling water or stock
  • 3 Tbspfinely chopped fresh parsley
  • 1 Tbspfresh lemon juice
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • opt. sidesteamed broccoli, arugula, or a green salad

Method

In a medium bowl, stir together the shrimp, 1 Tbsp of the oil, the lemon zest, red pepper flakes, ½ tsp salt, ¼ tsp black pepper, and half the garlic. Set aside to marinate. (Can be done up to 1 hour ahead, refrigerated.)
In a medium skillet, combine the butter, the remaining 2 Tbsp oil, and the remaining garlic over medium heat. When the butter bubbles, add the orzo and ½ tsp salt. Cook, stirring often, until the orzo is toasted, about 2 min, adjusting heat so the garlic doesn't burn.
Carefully add the ⅓ cup stock (it will bubble) and stir until absorbed, about 1 min. (This replaces the wine — you lose nothing important; the garlicky butter carries the dish.)
Stir in the 2 cups boiling water or stock, reduce the heat to low, cover, and cook until the orzo is al dente, about 12 min.
Add the shrimp in a snug, even layer on top of the orzo. Cover and cook until all the shrimp are pink and opaque throughout, 2–4 min. Pregnancy: make sure every shrimp is fully cooked — pink and firm, no translucent gray. Remove from heat and let sit, covered, 2 min.
Sprinkle with parsley and lemon juice, season with salt and pepper, and serve immediately.
Pregnancy Shrimp is low-mercury and safe in pregnancy when fully cooked — cook until pink and opaque throughout. The white wine is swapped for stock, so there's no alcohol. Shrimp brings some omega-3s and protein; the lemon adds vitamin C. A light, quick one-pan night.
Make ahead (once groceries arrive, ~10 min) Mince all the garlic; zest and juice the lemon; chop the parsley. Marinate the shrimp up to 1 hour ahead in the fridge. If your shrimp is frozen, thaw it in a bowl of cold water (~15 min) before marinating. The dish then comes together in about 20 minutes.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Shrimp and orzo are both good for kids. This has ½ tsp red pepper flakes — scoop a portion of orzo for the 13-mo before or use a light hand (it's mild but present). Chop the shrimp small for the 13-mo (a whole shrimp is a choking risk); if either kid hasn't had shellfish, introduce a small amount and watch for a couple of hours. The buttery orzo is usually an easy win.
Tuesday · Italian-American

Skillet "Pizza" Chicken with
Tomatoes & Mozzarella

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Melissa Clark. Scaled to ~2 lb chicken for the family.
45 mintotal
15 minactive
Oven 400°Fovenproof skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 2 lbbone-in, skin-on chicken thighs and/or drumsticks
  • 1¼ tspkosher salt (pantry)
  • ½ tspblack pepper (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 3 ozpancetta, diced
  • 3 clovesgarlic, thinly sliced
  • 2anchovy fillets
  • ¼ tspred pepper flakes (pantry)
  • 1 (28-oz) canwhole plum tomatoes
  • 1large basil sprig, plus chopped basil to serve
  • 6 ozbocconcini (halved) or fresh mozzarella, in ¾" pieces (pasteurized)
  • to servecrusty bread or rice, or a salad

Method

Heat the oven to 400°F. Pat the chicken dry and season both sides with the salt and pepper.
Heat the oil in a large ovenproof skillet over medium-high. Add the pancetta and cook, stirring, until browned. Transfer with a slotted spoon to a paper-towel-lined plate.
Add the chicken to the skillet and sear, turning occasionally, until well browned on all sides, about 10 min. Transfer to a plate. Pour off all but 1 Tbsp fat.
Add the garlic, anchovies, and red pepper flakes; fry, stirring, 1 min. Stir in the tomatoes and basil sprig, breaking up the tomatoes, and cook until slightly thickened, about 10 min.
Return the chicken to the skillet. Transfer to the oven and cook, uncovered, until the chicken is no longer pink, about 30 min. Pregnancy: chicken should reach 165°F.
Scatter the mozzarella over the top. Switch the oven to broil and broil until the cheese is melted and bubbling, 2–3 min (watch closely). Top with the crisp pancetta and chopped basil. Serve.
Pregnancy Chicken cooked fully to 165°F (the 30-min oven step handles it). Fresh mozzarella/bocconcini is safe when made from pasteurized milk — check the label — and it's melted under the broiler here anyway. Pancetta and anchovies are fully cooked. No wine in this sauce. A good iron source from the dark meat.
Make ahead (Monday evening, ~5 min) Slice the garlic; dice the pancetta; halve the bocconcini and refrigerate. The dish is mostly hands-off once it's in the oven, so there's little else to prep — the 30-min roast is a good window to handle bath/bedtime.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo "Pizza chicken" is a reliable kid hit — tomato and melty cheese. For the 13-mo: shred a piece of chicken off the bone (check for small bones and remove skin), spoon on a little sauce and cheese. The sauce has anchovy and a little heat but reads as mild tomato; taste first. For the 34-mo, serve with bread to dip in the sauce.

Coconut Fish & Tomato Bake

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Yewande Komolafe.
20 min active+ 15–30 min marinate
Oven 425°F + broilersheet pan
4 servings+ toddler portions
Low-mercurycod, snapper, or salmon

Ingredients

  • ¾ cupunsweetened coconut milk
  • 1 Tbsphoney
  • 1 (1") piecefresh ginger, scrubbed & grated
  • 1 clovegarlic, grated
  • ½ tspground turmeric
  • ½ tspred pepper flakes (pantry)
  • 2limes
  • ½ cupchopped fresh cilantro (divided)
  • 4 (6 oz)fish fillets — snapper, cod, haddock, striped bass, or salmon (low-mercury)
  • 2 pintscherry or grape tomatoes (~4 cups)
  • 3 Tbspolive oil (pantry)
  • —kosher salt (pantry)
  • to servesteamed rice or toasted baguette (pantry)

Method

In a large bowl, whisk the coconut milk, honey, ginger, garlic, turmeric, red pepper flakes, and 1 tsp salt. Zest and juice 1 lime directly into the mixture. Stir in ¼ cup of the cilantro.
Add the fish fillets and turn to coat. Cover and marinate in the fridge 15–30 min. (Good window to prep rice and set the table.)
Position one oven rack in the lower-middle and one as close to the broiler as possible. Heat the oven to 425°F.
Place the tomatoes on a large sheet pan, drizzle with 2 Tbsp of the oil, season with salt, and toss. Nestle the marinated fish among the tomatoes and spoon all the marinade over the fish. Drizzle the remaining 1 Tbsp oil over the top.
Roast on the lower-middle rack until the surface of the fish is opaque but the center isn't quite done, 8–10 min (depends on thickness). Then heat the broiler.
Broil on the top rack, rotating the pan halfway, until the fish is cooked through and flakes easily (145°F) and the tomatoes brown in spots, 5–6 min. Pregnancy: cook the fish fully through — opaque and flaking, not translucent in the center.
Divide the tomatoes and fish among plates; tip the pan juices over the fish. Garnish with the remaining ¼ cup cilantro and serve with lime wedges, over rice or with bread.
Pregnancy Choose a low-mercury fish — cod, snapper, haddock, striped bass, and salmon all qualify and all work here. Cook fully to 145°F (the broiler step handles it). Coconut milk, turmeric, ginger are all fine. No alcohol. The tomatoes and lime add vitamin C; a light, saucy, comforting night.
Make ahead (Tuesday evening, ~5 min) Whisk the coconut marinade (through the cilantro) and refrigerate. Grate the ginger and garlic. You can even add the fish to marinate the morning of — up to a few hours is fine. Wednesday is then just assembling the sheet pan and roasting.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo One NYT commenter's picky 3-year-old sopped up the sauce — the coconut-tomato sauce is mild and a little sweet. This has ½ tsp red pepper flakes in the marinade; it stays gentle, but pull a piece of fish before broiling if you want zero heat for the 13-mo. Flake the fish carefully for pin bones (especially with salmon or snapper). Serve over soft rice to catch the sauce. Honey is fine here for both kids since they're over 1.

Pregnancy food-safety, the working rules

  • Seafood: shrimp cooked until pink and opaque; fish to 145°F. Low-mercury choices only (cod, snapper, haddock, salmon — yes; swordfish, king mackerel, marlin, bigeye tuna — no).
  • Poultry: chicken to 165°F, no pink.
  • Cheese: fresh mozzarella fine if pasteurized (check label), and it's melted here.
  • Cured pork & anchovy: pancetta and anchovies fully cooked into the dish — safe.
  • No alcohol — Monday's wine is swapped for stock.

General guidance, not medical advice — defer to your OB/midwife.

Nutrition this week

  • Omega-3 (DHA): shrimp (Mon) and fish (Wed) — low-mercury seafood, good for fetal brain development.
  • Iron: dark-meat chicken (Tue); shrimp adds a little.
  • Absorption tip: the lemon (Mon), tomatoes (Tue, Wed), and lime (Wed) provide vitamin C to boost iron uptake.

Working with the kids

  • Mon (shrimp scampi): chop shrimp small for the 13-mo; buttery orzo is a win; watch shellfish if new.
  • Tue (pizza chicken): shred chicken off the bone, check for bones; tomato + cheese is a hit.
  • Wed (coconut fish): mild sweet sauce; flake fish for pin bones; serve over soft rice.

Substitutions, if Fresh is missing something

  • Orzo (Mon): any small pasta, or serve the scampi over rice or with crusty bread.
  • Bone-in chicken (Tue): bone-in breasts work; adjust roast so they reach 165°F. Boneless thighs also fine (shorter roast).
  • Bocconcini (Tue): any pasteurized fresh mozzarella, torn.
  • Fish (Wed): cod, snapper, haddock, striped bass, or salmon all work — pick what looks best and is low-mercury.
  • Coconut milk (Wed): full-fat is richest; light works with a slightly thinner sauce.

No seafood — and easy on a queasy stomach

Sorry the fish smell has been rough — that's a very common pregnancy aversion, and it often sharpens in the second trimester. This whole week is seafood-free. Friday's crispy gnocchi is deliberately mild and low-aroma (browned butter, tomato, cheese) in case a bland night is welcome. If a dish smells off on a given day, the gnocchi can move to whichever night sounds most tolerable — swap freely. Your 2 lb of skin-on drumsticks go into Thursday's Georgian chicken (a clean swap for the thighs the recipe calls for).

Pregnancy-safe throughout

Beef cooked fully through (a change from the recipe's rare), pork to 145°F+ with no pink, chicken to 175°F, mozzarella pasteurized and melted. No alcohol in any dish. Beef and chicken bring iron; walnuts add protein; the tomato-based dishes add vitamin C to help iron absorption.

A few pregnancy tweaks

Beef (Mon): cooked fully through, no pink, rather than left rare — the thin slices still stay tender. Pork (Tue): cooked to 145°F with no pink and a short rest, rather than the recipe's "barely pink" 140°F. Cotija (Tue): often unpasteurized — use a pasteurized cotija or swap in queso fresco/feta labeled pasteurized (or skip it). Drumsticks (Thu): cook to 175°F; being denser near the bone, they may want a few extra oven minutes vs. thighs.

If nausea is flaring

  • Friday's gnocchi is the blandest, lowest-smell option — move it earlier if needed.

Beef Short Rib Rice Bowls

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Sue Li. Beef cooked through for pregnancy.
20 mintotal (+ 5 min marinate)
20 minactive
Stovetoplarge skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 1 lbboneless beef short ribs, sliced ¼" thick (or skirt/hanger steak)
  • 2 Tbspsoy sauce (pantry)
  • 1 Tbsppeeled, grated fresh ginger
  • 1 Tbsplight brown sugar
  • 2 clovesgarlic, grated
  • 1 tspground coriander
  • 1 tspground cumin
  • ½ tspground turmeric
  • ¼ tspred pepper flakes (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspneutral oil (pantry)
  • —kosher salt (pantry)
  • to servesteamed rice (pantry)
  • to servelettuce leaves, sliced celery, cilantro, lime wedges

Method

Combine the short ribs, soy sauce, ginger, brown sugar, garlic, coriander, cumin, turmeric, red pepper flakes, and 1 tsp salt in a bowl; toss to coat. Cover and marinate in the fridge at least 5 min (up to 8 hours).
Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Working in batches so the pan isn't crowded, cook the beef until browned and cooked through — no pink, about 3 min per side.
Meanwhile, have the rice hot and ready, and prep the lettuce, celery, and cilantro.
Divide the rice and beef among bowls. Top with lettuce, celery, and cilantro. Serve with lime wedges to squeeze over.
Pregnancy Cook the beef fully through (no pink) rather than leaving it rare. Sliced thin, it cooks in a few minutes and stays tender. A strong iron night, with the lime adding vitamin C to help absorption. No alcohol. If the smell of searing beef is a trigger, have the other adult run the stovetop step — the assembled bowl is barely aromatic.
Make ahead (once groceries arrive, ~10 min) The marinade takes 2 minutes — you can toss the beef in it in the morning (or up to 8 hours ahead) so Monday is just sear + assemble. Slice the celery and wash the lettuce ahead. Start the rice first thing.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Tender beef over soft rice is easy for both kids. There's ¼ tsp red pepper flakes and soy in the marinade — mild, but you can rinse a piece of beef or set aside a portion. Cut the beef into small strips for the 13-mo. Lettuce cups are fun for the 34-mo to build their own bowl.
Tuesday · Mexican

Pork Chops in Chipotle Sauce
Chuletas de Puerco Enchipotladas

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights by Christopher Kimball (2018). Pork cooked to 145°F for pregnancy.
30 mintotal
25 minactive
Stovetop12" skillet + blender
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 1 lbplum tomatoes, cored & cut into chunks
  • 1 mediumwhite onion, cut into chunks
  • 4 clovesgarlic, peeled
  • 4chipotle chilies in adobo + sauce clinging to them, plus more adobo as needed
  • 1 tspground cumin
  • ¾ tspdried oregano (pantry)
  • 4center-cut bone-in pork chops, ~8 oz each, ½" thick, patted dry
  • 1 Tbspneutral oil (pantry)
  • 1½ ozcotija cheese, crumbled (pasteurized; or skip)
  • to servefresh cilantro; warm tortillas or roasted potatoes
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)

Method

In a blender, puree the tomatoes, onion, garlic, chipotles, cumin, and 1 tsp salt until smooth, about 1 min.
Make a couple of shallow vertical cuts in the silver skin around each chop (prevents curling), without cutting into the meat. Season the chops both sides with salt and pepper.
In a 12" skillet over medium-high, heat the oil until barely smoking. Brown 2 chops on one side, about 3 min, and transfer browned-side-up to a plate. Repeat with the other 2. Let the empty skillet cool 2–3 min.
Add the tomato-chipotle puree and oregano to the skillet. Cook over medium, stirring and scraping up browned bits, until slightly thickened, 10–15 min.
Return the chops (browned side up) and juices, nestling them into the sauce and spooning sauce over to submerge. Cook uncovered until the pork reaches 145°F with no pink, about 5–7 min, then rest 3 min. (The recipe stops at 140°F/barely pink; take it to 145°F for pregnancy.)
Transfer chops to a platter. Season the sauce; add more adobo for extra heat if you like. Spoon sauce over and around, then top with the cotija (pasteurized) and cilantro.
Pregnancy Cook the pork to 145°F with a 3-minute rest and no pink (a touch past the recipe's 140°F). Cotija is often made from unpasteurized milk — use a pasteurized cotija, sub queso fresco or feta labeled pasteurized, or leave it off. No alcohol. The tomatoes add vitamin C, and pork brings iron and B-vitamins.
Make ahead (Monday evening, ~5 min) Blend the tomato-chipotle sauce and refrigerate (it keeps a couple of days). Nick the silver skin on the chops. Tuesday is then brown + simmer + finish, about 25 min. The leftover chipotles in adobo freeze well in a small bag.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Four chipotles make a moderate heat that may be too much for the kids. Easiest path: set aside a little plain browned pork before it simmers, or blend a small portion of tomato/onion/garlic without the chipotles for their sauce. Cut the pork small and off the bone for the 13-mo. Serve with soft tortilla strips or potato.
Veggie side · adapted, not from a book Charred Corn & Zucchini Sauté

2 cups corn (fresh or frozen) · 2 zucchini, diced · ½ red onion · 1 clove garlic · 1 Tbsp butter or oil · juice of ½ lime · chopped cilantro · salt

Heat 1 Tbsp butter or oil in a skillet over medium-high. Add the corn in a single layer and let it sit undisturbed 2–3 min to char, then stir. Add the zucchini and onion; cook 4–5 min until just tender and lightly browned. Add the garlic for the last 30 sec. Off heat, stir in lime juice, cilantro, and salt. Mild, quick (~12 min), and it shares the cilantro and lime already on the list. A pinch of the pork's chipotle adobo stirred in makes it smoky if you want — leave it out for the kids and the mildest version. Both corn and zucchini are toddler-friendly; halve or cut the kernels for the 13-mo.
Thursday · Georgian

Georgian Chicken with
Walnut-Cilantro Sauce

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights by Christopher Kimball (2018). Using your 2 lb skin-on drumsticks (a direct swap for the thighs the recipe calls for).
40–45 mintotal
20 minactive
Stovetop + oven 450°Foven-safe 12" skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 4 tspground coriander, divided
  • 2 lbskin-on chicken drumsticks (your drumsticks)
  • 3 clovesgarlic, peeled
  • 3 Tbsptomato paste
  • 2serrano chilies, stemmed & seeded (or 1, or omit, for milder)
  • 3 Tbsppomegranate molasses, divided
  • ¾ cupfresh cilantro, divided
  • 1 cupwalnuts, toasted, divided
  • 1¼ cupslow-sodium chicken broth, divided
  • 1 Tbspgrapeseed or other neutral oil
  • —kosher salt (pantry)
  • to servewarmed flatbread and/or rice (pantry)

Method

Heat the oven to 450°F with a rack in the middle. Stir together 2 tsp of the coriander and 1½ tsp salt; season the drumsticks all over. Set aside.
In a blender, combine the garlic, tomato paste, chilies, 2 Tbsp of the pomegranate molasses, the remaining 2 tsp coriander, ½ cup of the cilantro, ¾ cup of the walnuts, ¾ cup of the broth, and 1 tsp salt. Puree until smooth, 30–45 seconds.
In an oven-safe 12" skillet over medium-high, heat the oil until barely smoking. Add the drumsticks and brown, turning, until the skin is crisp and golden on the main faces, 6–8 min total. (Drumsticks are rounder than thighs, so turn them to brown the meatier sides.) Transfer to a plate. Pour off the fat.
Return the skillet to medium-low; add the remaining ½ cup broth, scraping up browned bits. Add the walnut puree and bring to a simmer over medium. Nestle the drumsticks back in, keeping the tops above the sauce so the skin stays crisp.
Cook uncovered in the oven until a skewer in the thickest drumstick meets no resistance, about 18–22 min. Pregnancy: cook to 175°F — drumsticks are denser around the bone, so they need a few more minutes than thighs would; don't rush this.
Rest the drumsticks 5 min. Simmer the sauce on the stovetop with any accumulated juices until smooth and thick, about 1 min; season. Spoon the sauce onto a platter, nestle the drumsticks in, drizzle with the remaining 1 Tbsp pomegranate molasses, and sprinkle with the remaining ¼ cup walnuts and ¼ cup cilantro.
Pregnancy Cook the drumsticks fully to 175°F — dark meat is best there anyway, and being denser near the bone they want a few extra oven minutes vs. thighs. All ingredients pregnancy-safe. Walnuts add protein, healthy fats, and a little iron. No alcohol. Fragrant from coriander and pomegranate but not fishy or heavy; can be made largely by the other adult if aromas are an issue.
Make ahead (Wednesday evening, ~10 min) Make the entire walnut-cilantro puree and refrigerate. Toast the walnuts (reserve ¼ cup for garnish). Season the chicken. Thursday is then sear + oven + finish, about 20 min active.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo The walnut sauce is rich and mild if you go light on the chilies — use 1 or omit for a kid-friendly batch. Pull the meat off the drumstick bones (remove skin and check for cartilage/small bones) for the 13-mo. Whole walnuts are a choking hazard under 4 — the sauce blends them smooth, which is fine, but keep the garnish walnuts off the kids' plates. Serve with soft flatbread torn into strips.
Veggie side · adapted, not from a book Cucumber, Tomato & Herb Salad

2 Persian (or 1 English) cucumbers · 2 tomatoes · ¼ red onion, thin · handful cilantro + parsley · 1 Tbsp olive oil · 1 tsp red wine vinegar · salt

A cool, crunchy counter to the rich walnut sauce — and a genuinely traditional pairing with Georgian food. Chop the cucumbers and tomatoes into bite-size pieces, thinly slice the onion, and toss with torn herbs, olive oil, vinegar, and salt. Best dressed just before serving so it stays crisp. No cook, ~10 min, and it uses the cilantro already on the list. Halve the tomatoes and cut cucumber into soft small pieces for the 13-mo; skip the onion on their portion.
Friday · Italian · Vegetarian

Crispy Gnocchi with Burst
Tomatoes & Mozzarella

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Ali Slagle.
25 mintotal
25 minactive
Broilerlarge skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 2 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil, plus more (pantry)
  • 2 (12–18 oz) pkgsshelf-stable or refrigerated potato gnocchi
  • 4 Tbspunsalted butter
  • 4 clovesgarlic, thinly sliced
  • ¼ tspred pepper flakes, plus more (pantry)
  • 2 pintscherry or grape tomatoes (~4 cups)
  • ¼ cupsliced or torn fresh basil, plus more (optional)
  • 8 ozfresh mozzarella, in ½" pieces (pasteurized)
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • opt.½ tsp sugar if tomatoes are tart

Method

Heat the broiler with a rack about 6" from the heat.
Heat 1 Tbsp of the oil in a large or cast-iron skillet over medium-high. Add half the gnocchi in a single layer, breaking apart any stuck together. Cover and cook undisturbed until golden brown on the underside, 2–4 min. Transfer to a bowl. Repeat with 1 Tbsp oil and the rest of the gnocchi.
Add the butter to the skillet over medium-high and cook, stirring often, until golden brown and toasty, 1–2 min. Add the garlic, red pepper flakes, 1½ tsp salt, and a few grinds of pepper, lowering the heat to avoid scorching.
Add the tomatoes and 3 Tbsp water. Cook, shaking the pan occasionally, until the tomatoes soften and the liquid thickens slightly, 4–6 min, smashing the tomatoes as they burst. (Add ½ tsp sugar if they're tart.)
Return the gnocchi to the skillet and stir to coat. Nestle the mozzarella pieces in among the gnocchi. Transfer the skillet to the broiler and broil until the cheese is melted and bubbling, 2–4 min (watch closely).
Top with basil and more red pepper flakes if you like. Serve.
Pregnancy Vegetarian, mild, and low-aroma — a gentle option if the smell of cooking meat or fish is hard right now. Fresh mozzarella is safe when pasteurized (check the label) and it's melted here anyway. No alcohol. The tomatoes add vitamin C; the gnocchi are easy, plain carbs that tend to sit well with nausea.
Make ahead (little needed) This is a 25-minute dish with almost no prep — just slice the garlic and tear the mozzarella ahead if you want. Best made fresh so the gnocchi stay crisp.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Crispy gnocchi and melty cheese are a near-guaranteed kid win. There's ¼ tsp red pepper flakes in the whole pan — mild, but you can scoop a portion before adding them for the 13-mo. Cut the gnocchi in halves or quarters for the 13-mo (they can be a choking risk whole) and make sure the cheese has cooled enough. Halve the tomatoes for both kids.

Pregnancy food-safety, the working rules

  • Meat & poultry: beef through/no pink (Mon), pork to 145°F + rest (Tue), drumsticks to 175°F (Thu).
  • Cheese: cotija must be pasteurized (or sub/skip); mozzarella pasteurized and melted.
  • No alcohol in any of these dishes.
  • Wash produce well, especially the tomatoes and herbs.
  • No seafood this week — per the aversion.

General guidance, not medical advice — defer to your OB/midwife.

Managing the nausea / aversion

  • Friday's gnocchi is the mildest, lowest-aroma night — move it earlier in the week if a bland meal is what sounds doable.
  • Cooking smells are often the worst part — let the non-pregnant adult sear the beef and pork and brown the drumsticks; open a window or run the vent.
  • Room-temp or cool leftovers smell much less than hot food.
  • Plain carbs (rice, gnocchi) and cold, tart, or ginger-forward things tend to sit better; keep easy snacks on hand.

Working with the kids

  • Mon (beef bowls): tender beef over soft rice; rinse a piece or set aside a portion before the spicier marinade for the 13-mo; cut small.
  • Tue (pork chops): 4 chipotles is moderate heat — set aside plain browned pork or blend a chile-free sauce portion for the kids; cut off the bone.
  • Thu (drumsticks): go light on the chilies for a kid batch; pull meat off the bones; keep garnish walnuts off kid plates.
  • Fri (gnocchi): a reliable hit; halve/quarter gnocchi and tomatoes for the 13-mo.

Substitutions, if Fresh is missing something

  • Short ribs (Mon): skirt or hanger steak, sliced thin, works just as well.
  • Pork chops (Tue): ¾–1" chops work — brown both sides and simmer 7–9 min. Boneless loin chops also fine.
  • Cotija (Tue): pasteurized queso fresco or feta, or leave it off.
  • Pomegranate molasses (Thu): 2 Tbsp balsamic + 1 tsp honey approximates the sweet-tart note.
  • Drumsticks (Thu): bone-in thighs are the original; either way cook dark meat to 175°F.
  • Gnocchi (Fri): shelf-stable or refrigerated both work; cauliflower gnocchi also crisps well.
  • Serranos (Thu): jalapeño (milder) or omit for no heat.

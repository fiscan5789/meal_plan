Monday · Japanese

Chicken Katsu
with Cabbage Slaw

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Kay Chun.
30 mintotal
30 minactive (mostly assembly)
Stovetopcast iron or heavy skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Tonkatsu sauce (make ahead Sunday)

  • 6 Tbspketchup
  • 6 TbspWorcestershire sauce
  • 4 tspunsulfured molasses
  • 2 tsplow-sodium soy sauce (pantry)
  • 2 tspgranulated sugar
  • ¼ tsppeeled, grated fresh ginger
  • ⅛ tspground cloves

Katsu (Monday night)

  • ~1 cupvegetable or canola oil, for frying (depth of ⅓")
  • ½ cupall-purpose flour (pantry)
  • 2large eggs, beaten
  • 1½ cupspanko breadcrumbs (about 3½ oz)
  • 2boneless skinless chicken breasts (½ lb each), halved crosswise and pounded to ¼" thick
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)

Cabbage salad & serving

  • 12 ozfinely shredded green cabbage (about 4 packed cups — uses half a head)
  • 2 Tbsprice vinegar (pantry)
  • 1 Tbsptoasted sesame oil (pantry)
  • 1 tsplow-sodium soy sauce (pantry)
  • 1 tspgranulated sugar
  • 2scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsptoasted sesame seeds (pantry)
  • pinchkosher salt (pantry)
  • to servesteamed white rice (pantry)
  • to servelemon wedges

Method — Monday, 30 min

Start rice (if using). 18 min for jasmine rice in a covered saucepan; longer for brown.
Pour oil into a large cast-iron or heavy skillet to a depth of ⅓". Heat over medium until an instant-read thermometer registers 350°F. (Or test with a piece of bread — it should sizzle steadily and brown in ~30 seconds.)
Set up three wide shallow bowls: flour in one, beaten eggs in the second, panko in the third.
Season the 4 chicken cutlets on both sides with salt and pepper. Working with one at a time, dredge in flour (shake off excess), dip in egg (let excess drip off), then press firmly into the panko, coating both sides. Set aside on a clean plate. Repeat with remaining cutlets.
Gently lower 2 cutlets into the hot oil. Fry until golden on the underside, about 2 min. Adjust heat to keep oil close to 350°F — too hot, the breading burns before the chicken cooks; too cold, the breading gets greasy.
Flip the cutlets. Fry until golden on the second side and cooked through, another 1½–2 min. Transfer to a paper-towel-lined plate and season immediately with a pinch of salt.
Fry the remaining 2 cutlets the same way.
While the second batch fries, dress the cabbage: in a large bowl, whisk together rice vinegar, sesame oil, soy sauce, sugar, and a pinch of salt until the sugar dissolves. Add the shredded cabbage and sliced scallions; toss well so every shred gets coated. Let sit 2–3 minutes — the cabbage softens slightly and absorbs the dressing.
Cut each cutlet on the diagonal into thick slices. Plate with a generous mound of the dressed cabbage salad alongside and a small bowl of rice. Sprinkle the cabbage with sesame seeds. Drizzle the chicken with tonkatsu sauce; pass the rest at the table. Serve with lemon wedges.
The temperature thing 350°F is the goldilocks zone for breaded cutlets — high enough for fast browning, low enough that the panko stays crisp instead of burning. An instant-read or candy thermometer earns its keep here. If the oil cools too much when you drop cutlets in, the breading absorbs oil and gets soggy; if it spikes, the breading burns brown before the chicken interior is safe. Adjust the burner up and down between batches.
Make ahead (Sunday, 10 min) Mix the tonkatsu sauce — it lasts indefinitely in the fridge. Whisk the cabbage-salad dressing (rice vinegar, sesame oil, soy sauce, sugar, salt) in a small jar — keeps a week. Pound the chicken cutlets thin between sheets of plastic wrap (use the bottom of a heavy skillet or a rolling pin); store covered. Shred the cabbage (12 oz, about 4 cups packed) and store in a container with a damp paper towel — keeps crisp for 3 days. Slice the scallions and store with the cabbage (or separately). Crack the eggs into a small jar — will be ready Monday. Monday night is then just dredge, fry, dress the cabbage, plate.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Chicken katsu is a great kid food — crispy outside, tender inside, no spice. For both kids: cut into small strips for easy hand-eating. The 13-mo will need pieces small enough to gum if no teeth; the panko crust softens when you let it sit a minute. Tonkatsu sauce is fine for the 34-mo (it's sweet-tangy, not spicy) but skip it for the 13-mo — the soy sauce and Worcestershire make it salty. The dressed cabbage salad is a bit much for the 13-mo — pull a small amount of cabbage out before dressing and chop it very fine; soft-cooked cabbage or even plain steamed broccoli works better at that age. The 34-mo will likely eat the dressed cabbage; it's mild and slightly sweet.
Tuesday · Italian · Vegetarian-leaning

Pasta e Fagiole
Freezer-friendly

From Where Cooking Begins by Carla Lalli Music (2019). Her mother's recipe.
15 min day-of(base made Sunday)
15 minactive
Stovetoppot
4 + 4–6 frozenportions total

Soup base Sunday cook

  • 8 ozdried medium white beans (cannellini), soaked overnight if possible
  • 6 clovesgarlic
  • 4medium carrots, scrubbed, roughly chopped
  • 1leek (white and pale green only), halved, rinsed, roughly chopped
  • ⅓ cupextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 1smoked ham hock (or 4 oz pancetta or bacon ends)
  • 1 (15-oz) canwhole peeled tomatoes
  • 1 bunchkale, leaves stripped from stems
  • 1–2Parmigiano rinds (optional, big flavor boost if you have them)
  • 2bay leaves
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)

Tuesday night — just to finish

  • 8 ozsmall dried pasta (ditalini, small shells, pipette, orzo)
  • —kosher salt for the pasta water
  • to serveParmigiano for grating, crusty bread, crushed red pepper, olive oil

Sunday — cook the soup base

Power-soak the beans if you didn't soak overnight: put beans in a large pot, cover with water by 1", bring to a boil. As soon as it boils, turn off heat, stir in a palmful of salt, cover, and let sit for 1 hour. Drain.
In a food processor, combine the garlic, carrots, and leeks. Pulse until very finely chopped (small confetti).
In a soup pot or Dutch oven, heat ⅓ cup olive oil over medium. Add the chopped vegetable mix. Season generously with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring often, until the vegetables sweat out their liquid, 3–5 min. Don't brown them — just soften.
Cover the pot and cook over medium-low, stirring every 5 minutes, until the sofrito is very soft and juicy, about 15 min. Reduce heat if it starts to brown.
Add the ham hock (or pancetta) and cook uncovered, stirring and scraping the bottom of the pot every 5 min, until the sofrito starts to brown in places and has lost at least half its volume, about 10 min more.
Add the soaked beans plus water to cover by 2", the canned tomatoes (crush with your hands as you add), and the kale. Season with salt and pepper. Bring to a boil, then add the Parmigiano rinds (if using) and bay leaves. Reduce heat to a very gentle simmer.
Cook with the lid askew until the beans are very tender, 1 to 3 hours depending on the beans' age. Add water as needed to keep the beans submerged by at least 1". Taste and adjust salt; the soup should be well-seasoned.
Divide the soup: remove the ham hock (if you used one) — pull off any meat, shred it, return to the soup. Discard bones, bay leaves, Parmigiano rinds.
Reserve ~6 cups soup base for Tuesday in a large container. Portion the remaining 6–9 cups into 4–6 freezer-safe quart bags or containers (1½ cups each = one adult serving). Label "Pasta e Fagiole base, [date]". Freeze flat for stacking.

Tuesday — 15 min to dinner

Reheat the reserved 6 cups of soup base in a pot over medium-low, adding water (or stock if you have it) as needed to loosen — the base will have thickened in the fridge.
Meanwhile, bring a separate pot of well-salted water to a boil. Add the pasta and set a timer for 2–3 min less than the package time — you want very al dente. Drain.
Crucial: add the cooked pasta to the soup just before serving. Do not cook the pasta in the soup. The noodles will absorb all the liquid, get gummy, and ruin the leftovers.
Taste, adjust salt and pepper. Serve in bowls with grated Parm, a drizzle of olive oil, crushed red pepper, and bread for dunking.
Freezing & reheating the base The bean-and-vegetable base freezes for 3 months. To reheat a frozen portion: thaw overnight in the fridge, or place the frozen block in a pot with ¼ cup water over medium-low, covered, for 15 min. Add a fresh handful of cooked small pasta when warm. Never freeze the pasta in the soup — pasta-on-day-three is sad, pasta-after-freezing is worse.
Sunday prep timing Start the beans around 1pm if soaking the morning of, or earlier if you've soaked overnight. The 1–3 hour simmer is hands-off — you can do other prep while it cooks. Plan for the whole soup process to take ~3 hours start to finish, mostly passive.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Pasta e fagiole is one of the great kid-friendly soups: soft beans, tender pasta, mild flavor, easy to eat with a spoon. For the 13-mo: ladle a small bowl, mash the beans slightly with the back of a spoon, cut a few pasta pieces in half. Skip the red pepper flakes. For the 34-mo: an unmodified small portion is usually a hit. The Parm cheese on top is a kid win — just go light for the 13-mo (sodium).

Greek Pork Meatballs
in Tomato Sauce
Freezer-friendly

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights Mediterranean by Christopher Kimball (2021). Soutzoukakia smyrneika.
25 min day-of(meatballs shaped Sunday)
20 minactive
Stovetop12" nonstick skillet
4 servings+ raw frozen batch

Scaled to 1½ lb pork Shape 18 total, eat 12, freeze 6

  • ¾ cuppanko breadcrumbs
  • 1large egg + 1 yolk
  • ¾ cupwater
  • 1½ lbground pork
  • 1 Tbspground cumin
  • 4 clovesgarlic (3 finely grated, 1 thinly sliced)
  • 1 tspred pepper flakes, divided
  • 3 Tbspfinely chopped fresh oregano, divided
  • 1½ tspkosher salt + 1 tsp pepper for meatballs

Sauce (Wed night only)

  • 2 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil, plus more to serve (pantry)
  • 28-oz cancrushed tomatoes
  • ½ tspground cinnamon
  • 2 tsphoney
  • ½ tspsalt + ¼ tsp pepper for sauce

Greek cucumber-tomato salad (Wed night only)

  • 1English cucumber (or 2 Persian), diced into ½" pieces
  • 1 pintcherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼small red onion, very thinly sliced
  • 2 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspred wine vinegar
  • ½ tspdried oregano (pantry)
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • opt.crumbled feta, a small handful

To serve

  • opt.rice, orzo, or warm pita
  • opt.plain Greek yogurt on the side

Sunday — shape the meatballs (12 + 6 to freeze)

In a medium bowl, combine the panko, egg, yolk, and water. Mix until smooth. Let stand 5 min so the panko hydrates — this is the panade, and it gives the meatballs their tender texture. Don't skip this step.
Add the ground pork, cumin, 3 grated garlic cloves (save the sliced one for Wednesday's sauce), ½ tsp red pepper flakes (save the rest for Wed), 1½ Tbsp oregano (save the rest), 1½ tsp salt, and 1 tsp pepper. Mix well with your hands but don't overwork.
Divide into 18 portions (each a scant ¼ cup). Shape each into a 2½" cigar shape. Wet your hands slightly to prevent sticking.
For 12 (for Wednesday): arrange on a parchment-lined plate, cover with plastic, refrigerate.
For 6 (for the freezer): arrange on a parchment-lined small sheet pan or plate, freeze uncovered until solid (~2 hours), then transfer to a freezer bag. Label "Greek pork meatballs, raw, [date]". Keeps 2 months. To use later: thaw overnight in fridge, then cook from the "Method" step below.

Wednesday — 25 min to dinner

Heat 2 Tbsp olive oil in a 12" nonstick skillet over medium-high until shimmering.
Add the 12 meatballs and cook undisturbed until browned on the bottoms, 2–3 min. Using tongs, flip and brown the second sides, another 2–3 min. Transfer to a paper-towel-lined plate.
Return the skillet to medium-high. Add the sliced garlic clove. Cook, stirring, until fragrant and starting to brown, 1–2 min.
Add the remaining ½ tsp red pepper flakes; cook stirring 30 seconds. Stir in the crushed tomatoes, cinnamon, honey, ½ tsp salt, and ¼ tsp black pepper. Bring to a simmer.
Nestle the meatballs back into the sauce. Return to a simmer, cover, and cook undisturbed until the meatballs reach 160°F at center, 12–14 min.
While the meatballs simmer, make the salad: in a medium bowl, combine the diced cucumber, halved cherry tomatoes, and sliced red onion. Add olive oil, red wine vinegar, dried oregano, ½ tsp salt, and a few grinds of black pepper. Toss gently. Taste and adjust salt. (If you have feta, crumble it over the top just before serving.) Let sit until ready to plate — the tomatoes will release some of their juices, which makes the salad even better.
Transfer meatballs and sauce to a serving dish. Drizzle with additional olive oil and sprinkle with the remaining 1½ Tbsp oregano. Plate alongside the cucumber-tomato salad. Serve.
Why freeze raw, not cooked Raw meatballs freeze better than cooked: less freezer-burn risk, no soggy texture on thaw. To use the frozen batch later: thaw 6 in the fridge overnight, then proceed with the cooking method using a half-batch of sauce (14-oz can tomatoes instead of 28-oz; halve everything else in the sauce). 25 min to dinner from a freezer raid.
Sunday prep (~30 min) The panade hydration is 5 min, mixing is 5, shaping 18 cigars is ~15 min, flash-freezing 6 of them is hands-off. Total ~25 min for everything. Schedule this after the pasta e fagiole has its lid askew and is simmering. Salad prep is Wednesday only: cucumber and cherry tomatoes are best diced just before serving (they get watery if cut ahead), but you can thinly slice the red onion Tuesday night and store it covered — the bite mellows overnight.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Meatballs are gold for both kids. For the 13-mo: pull 2 meatballs from the pan before adding red pepper flakes to the sauce; finish them in plain crushed tomatoes (set aside half a cup at the start). Break the cooked meatballs apart with a fork to serve. For the 34-mo: a whole meatball with a little sauce is usually a hit; the cinnamon-honey-tomato combo is gentle and warming. For the salad: cherry tomatoes are an AAP choking hazard whole — quarter them for the 13-mo, halve for the 34-mo. Cucumber is fine for both ages in small dice. Skip the raw red onion for both kids (sharp); skip the vinegar dressing for the 13-mo (acidic) — offer a few plain cucumber and tomato pieces instead.
Thursday · Mediterranean

Lemony Shrimp
& Bean Stew

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Sue Li.
30 mintotal
25 minactive
Stovetoplarge pot
4 servings

Ingredients

  • 1 tspgrated lemon zest
  • 1 tspsweet or smoked paprika
  • 2 clovesgarlic, grated
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • 1 lblarge shrimp, peeled, deveined, tails removed
  • 2large leeks (or 1 large onion)
  • 4 Tbspunsalted butter
  • 1 (15-oz) cancannellini beans, drained and rinsed
  • 2 cupschicken or vegetable stock
  • 2 Tbspfinely chopped fresh parsley
  • 2 Tbspfresh lemon juice
  • to servetoasted bread

Method

In a medium bowl, mix the lemon zest, paprika, grated garlic, ¾ tsp salt, and ¾ tsp pepper. Add the shrimp, toss to coat, and set aside while you prep the leeks.
Trim the leeks: cut off the dark green tops and root ends. Halve lengthwise and cut the white and pale green parts into ½"-thick half-moons. Rinse thoroughly in a bowl of cold water to remove grit; drain. (If using onion instead, just mince it.)
Melt the butter in a large pot over medium-high heat. When foaming, add the shrimp. Cook, stirring occasionally, until pink and starting to curl, 2–3 min.
Use a slotted spoon to transfer the shrimp to a plate; set aside. Leave the butter and any liquid in the pot.
Reduce heat to medium. Add the leeks (or onion), season with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until soft and starting to brown at the edges, 4–5 min.
Add the cannellini beans and stock. Increase heat to high; bring to a boil. Lower to a simmer and cook 8–10 min — this lets the flavors marry and the broth thicken slightly.
Stir in the reserved shrimp (and any juices on the plate), the parsley, and the lemon juice. Season with salt and pepper. Serve immediately with toasted bread on the side.
Make ahead (Wednesday evening, ~5 min) Zest the lemon (store with a few drops of olive oil to prevent drying), juice the lemon (jar it), mix the shrimp spice blend (lemon zest + paprika + garlic + salt + pepper) in a small jar. If using shell-on shrimp: peel, devein, tail-off Wednesday night; store on a paper-towel-lined plate, covered. Trim and slice the leeks; store in a container.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Shrimp is fine developmentally for both kids; if either hasn't had shellfish before, introduce small and watch for 2 hours. For the 13-mo: chop a couple of cooked shrimp into very small pieces; serve with a spoonful of broth and a few mashed beans. For the 34-mo: whole or halved shrimp with bread for dunking is usually a hit. Skip the lemon juice for the 13-mo (acid is sharp); the toddler version is gentler. The paprika is mild enough for both.

Spicy Pork & Black
Bean Chili
Freezer-friendly

From Dinner by Melissa Clark (2017).
10 min day-of(chili made Sunday)
10 minactive
Stovetopreheat in pot
6–8 frozen Sunthaw 4–5 Wed for Fri

Scaled to 1½ lb pork Eat ~4 portions Fri, freeze 2–4

  • 3 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 1 largewhite onion, diced
  • 1poblano chile or red bell pepper, diced
  • 1½ lbground pork
  • 3 tspkosher salt, plus more to taste (pantry)
  • 1½ tspfreshly ground black pepper (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspdried oregano
  • 1½ Tbspfresh sage, minced (or 2 tsp dried)
  • 1½ Tbspchili powder
  • 3 clovesgarlic, minced
  • 28-oz candiced tomatoes
  • 2 (15-oz) cansblack beans, rinsed and drained
  • 1 cuplager beer (Negra Modelo or similar) — or substitute chicken stock

To serve (Fri night)

  • opt.grated white cheddar or sour cream
  • —lime wedges
  • opt.cooked rice or warm cornbread on the side

Sunday — cook the chili (~1 hour)

Heat the olive oil in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat.
Add the diced onion and poblano (or bell pepper). Cook, stirring, until softened and lightly browned, about 7 min.
Add the ground pork. Break it up with a wooden spoon and cook until well-browned, another 7 min. The pork should release fat and brown in places.
Stir in the salt, pepper, oregano, sage, chili powder, and minced garlic. Cook 1 minute until fragrant.
Add the diced tomatoes (with all their liquid), the black beans, and the beer (or stock). Stir well, scraping the bottom of the pot to lift any browned bits. Bring to a boil.
Reduce heat to medium-low and simmer until slightly thickened, 30–40 min. Stir occasionally. Taste and adjust salt and pepper at the end.
Cool to room temperature. Portion all of it for the freezer. Divide into 6–8 freezer bags (1½–2 cups each = one adult serving; ~6 total cups for Friday's dinner + 4–8 cups for future weeks). Label "Pork chili, [date]". Freeze flat for stacking. Important: don't hold any in the fridge — Sunday-to-Friday is too long for a cooked meat dish to sit refrigerated.
Wednesday evening (sets up Friday): move 4–5 freezer bags (~6 cups total) from the freezer to the fridge to thaw slowly. By Friday they'll be fully thawed and ready to reheat.

Friday — 10 min to dinner

Empty the thawed chili bags into a pot. Reheat over medium-low, stirring occasionally, until heated through — 10–12 min. Add a splash of water or stock if it has thickened too much.
Taste, adjust salt. Serve in bowls with cheese, sour cream, lime wedges, and rice or cornbread alongside.
Freezing & reheating Chili is the platonic freezer meal — the spices deepen, the textures stay intact, and the reheat is foolproof. Keeps 3 months. To reheat a frozen portion: thaw in the fridge overnight, then warm in a pot over medium-low; or warm from frozen in a covered pot with ¼ cup added water over medium-low for 20 min, stirring occasionally to break up the block.
Sunday prep timing The chili is your last Sunday cook of the day. Start it after the meatballs are shaped and the pasta e fagiole is at its lid-askew simmer. 20 min of active work (sauteing, browning), then 30–40 min hands-off simmer.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo The chili powder is spicy for a 13-month-old. Best approach: when cooking on Sunday, set aside a 1-cup portion before adding the chili powder — just season with salt and the milder spices. Simmer separately in a small pot for the 30 min; freeze if not using right away. This gives you a kid-safe baseline of 4–5 toddler portions for the week. For the 34-mo: a small portion of the regular chili thinned with sour cream usually goes down well. Beans are easy chew for both kids; ground pork is well-broken-down. Skip the cheese sprinkle for the 13-mo (sodium).

What's now in your freezer

  • Pasta e Fagiole base: 4–6 portions, 1½ cups each. Keeps 3 months.
  • Pork & Black Bean Chili: 6–8 total portions in the freezer Sunday. You'll thaw 4–5 of them Wednesday to eat Friday; the remaining 2–4 stay in the freezer for future weeks. Keeps 3 months.

Label everything with name + date in Sharpie on the freezer bag. Use within 3 months for best texture.

Reheating any frozen portion

  • From thawed (overnight in fridge): warm in a pot over medium-low, 8–10 min, stirring.
  • From frozen: place block in a pot with ¼ cup water, cover, medium-low heat, 15–20 min, breaking up with a spoon as it melts.
  • Microwave (less ideal but fast): covered bowl, 50% power, stirring every 2 min.
  • Frozen raw meatballs: thaw overnight in fridge, then proceed with the recipe's cooking method. Don't sear from frozen — they'll burn on the outside before the inside cooks.

Working with the kids

  • Mon (Chicken katsu): kid-friendly by default — crispy chicken, no spice. For the 13-mo: cut into small strips, skip the tonkatsu sauce (too salty). For the 34-mo: serve with a small dollop of tonkatsu for dipping.
  • Tue (Pasta e fagiole): straightforward kid food. Mash a few beans for the 13-mo; halve a few pasta pieces.
  • Wed (Greek meatballs): the cinnamon-honey-tomato sauce is gentle. Make sure to set aside 2 meatballs for the 13-mo before the red pepper flakes go in the sauce.
  • Thu (Shrimp stew): watch for shellfish allergy if either kid hasn't had it.
  • Fri (Pork chili): the chili powder is too spicy for the 13-mo. Reserve a 1-cup portion of the pork-and-bean mixture before adding chili powder during Sunday's cook — freeze separately as "kid chili".

Substitutions, if Fresh is missing something

  • Tonkatsu sauce shortcut: bottled tonkatsu (often labeled "Bull-Dog brand") is in many supermarkets. Tastes great. Skip making it from scratch if you'd rather.
  • Smoked ham hock: 4 oz pancetta, bacon ends, or a smoked turkey wing. Last resort: a Parmigiano rind plus 1 Tbsp tomato paste for umami depth.
  • Dried cannellini beans: 2 cans cannellini, drained and rinsed (skip the soaking step, add directly to the sofrito with about 4 cups water).
  • Poblano: red bell pepper (less smoky, fine), or jalapeno (hotter).
  • Lager beer: chicken stock plus 1 tsp brown sugar gets close.
  • Ditalini / small pasta: any small shape works. Avoid spaghetti or linguine (broken into pieces is OK but ditalini is much better).

The shape of the half-week

Big flavor on Wednesday (Kenji's pan-sear plus a quick mushroom side using the same skillet), then two oven-driven nights so you're hands-free for kid bath/bedtime. Beef → salmon → chicken keeps the protein rotation tight, and three different finishing flavor profiles (French-bistro herb butter, Asian sesame-ginger, Japanese miso-honey) so nothing tastes the same.

The 12 oz strip steak — important note

One 12 oz steak is enough beef for two adults to share generously; the toddler will eat a slice or two; the 13-month-old isn't really a beef eater yet. The mushroom side bulks up the plate; the arugula salad makes it feel complete. If you'd rather scale up to feed everyone substantially on beef alone, swap to two 8 oz or two 12 oz steaks and follow the same method—just cook in batches in the skillet.

Wednesday · French bistro

Pan-Seared Strip Steak
with Mushrooms & Arugula

Steak method & mushroom side adapted from The Food Lab by J. Kenji López-Alt. Salad is a simple lemon-shallot dressing—the classic steak-frites side, minus the frites.
25 mintotal (plus 45 min salt-rest)
~25 minactive
Cast ironor stainless skillet
2 adults + toddler bites1 × 12 oz steak

Steak

  • 1 × 12 ozboneless strip steak, 1–1¼" thick
  • —kosher salt & freshly ground pepper (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspneutral oil (canola, grapeseed, or vegetable)
  • 1 Tbspunsalted butter
  • 2 sprigsfresh thyme
  • 1shallot, finely sliced

Pan-Roasted Mushrooms

  • 1 Tbspneutral oil
  • 12 ozcremini mushrooms, quartered (or sliced thick if large)
  • 1shallot, minced
  • 2 clovesgarlic, minced or grated
  • 1 tspfresh thyme leaves
  • 1 tspsoy sauce (pantry)
  • ½ tspfresh lemon juice
  • 1 Tbspunsalted butter
  • —salt & pepper

Arugula Salad

  • 5 ozbaby arugula (one clamshell)
  • 2 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspfresh lemon juice (~½ lemon)
  • ¼ tspDijon mustard (optional, helps emulsify)
  • —flaky salt & pepper
  • opt.shaved Parmigiano-Reggiano

Method

Tomorrow (Tue) or 45 min before you start cooking: Pat the steak completely dry. Salt all sides generously and let it sit, uncovered, on a rack over a plate in the fridge—or on the counter for at least 45 min. (Dry surface = better crust. Salt early = better seasoning all the way through.)
Get organized first. Quarter the mushrooms, mince the shallots (one for the steak, one for the mushrooms), mince the garlic, pluck the thyme leaves. Wash and spin-dry the arugula. Whisk the salad dressing in the salad bowl bottom: olive oil, lemon juice, Dijon, salt, pepper.
Cook the mushrooms. Heat 1 Tbsp oil in a large nonstick skillet over high heat until shimmering. Add the mushrooms and cook, tossing frequently, until they release their liquid and it fully evaporates—about 8 minutes. Don't panic when they look wet; this always happens. Keep cooking, tossing, until deep brown, another 4–6 minutes.
Add the minced shallot, garlic, and thyme. Toss for 30 seconds until fragrant. Off the heat: stir in the soy sauce, lemon juice, and butter until the butter melts. Salt and pepper to taste. Slide into a serving bowl, cover loosely with foil to keep warm.
Cook the steak. Wipe out the mushroom pan (or grab your cast iron if you'd prefer). Pat the steak dry one more time. Heat 1 Tbsp oil in a 10–12" skillet over high heat until just smoking.
Carefully add the steak. Cook, flipping every 30–60 seconds (yes, frequently—Kenji's research shows this gives a better crust and more even cook), until both sides have a light brown crust, about 4 minutes total.
Reduce the heat to medium. Add the butter, thyme sprigs, and sliced shallot. Continue flipping and basting with the foaming butter (tilt the pan, spoon the butter back over) until an instant-read thermometer in the center reads 120°F for medium-rare or 125°F for medium—about 3 to 4 more minutes for a 1" steak. Don't trust time; trust the thermometer.
Rest. Transfer the steak to a board or plate, tent loosely with foil. Rest 5 minutes—non-negotiable, juices need to redistribute. The internal temp will rise 5–8°F as it rests.
Plate. Toss the arugula with the dressing. Slice the steak against the grain in ½" slices. Plate: arugula on one side, mushrooms next to it, steak slices over the arugula. Pour any juices from the board over the steak. Shave Parm over the salad if using.
Why the flip-frequently method Conventional wisdom says flip a steak only once. Kenji tested it: flipping every 30–60 seconds cooks the interior more evenly and develops crust just as well, because each side gets brief, intense exposures separated by short recoveries—essentially gentler convection. The "gray band" of overcooked meat just under the crust shrinks. For a thin steak like this 12 oz strip, the difference is meaningful.
Make ahead (Tue evening, ~10 min) Pat the steak dry, salt it generously, set on a rack over a plate, uncovered, in the fridge. Sitting uncovered for 24 hours produces an even drier surface and even better crust. Quarter the mushrooms. Wash and dry the arugula thoroughly, store wrapped in a paper towel. Whisk the salad dressing in a jar.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo 34-month-old: Slice a few thin pieces of medium-rare steak (still pink, fine for toddlers if cooked through to 120°F+). Mushrooms—give them whole or halved; most kids love the umami. Skip the arugula or offer a few leaves as "tree leaves" if they're feeling adventurous.

13-month-old: Beef can be tough for a 13-mo. Best bet: slice a piece very thin across the grain, then chop very fine, almost shredded. Offer 1–2 small mushrooms cut into ¼" pieces (low salt for them—the soy sauce dose is small, but if you're sodium-cautious, pull out a few unseasoned mushrooms before you add the soy/butter and serve those instead).
Thursday · Asian-inspired

Sesame-Ginger Salmon
& Broccoli

Adapted from Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking) by Lidey Heuck
25 mintotal
10 minactive
Oven 425°Fsheet pan
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 4 Tbsptoasted sesame oil, divided
  • 2 Tbspsoy sauce (pantry)
  • 1 Tbsprice vinegar
  • 1 Tbsphoney
  • 2 Tbspfresh ginger, peeled & grated (about a 2" piece)
  • 1garlic clove, grated
  • 1 lbbroccoli, cut into florets (thick stems trimmed off)
  • 2scallions, cut into 1½" pieces (plus more thinly sliced, for finish)
  • 1 Tbspolive oil (pantry)
  • 4 (6 oz)skin-on salmon fillets
  • ½lime, for serving
  • —sesame seeds, kosher salt, black pepper

Method

Heat the oven to 425°F. Whisk 3 Tbsp of the sesame oil with the soy sauce, rice vinegar, honey, ginger, and garlic until smooth. This is your glaze—set it aside.
On a rimmed sheet pan, toss the broccoli florets and 1½" scallion segments with the olive oil and remaining 1 Tbsp sesame oil. Season with ½ tsp salt and ¼ tsp pepper. Spread in a single layer and roast for 5 minutes.
While the broccoli starts, pat the salmon dry, brush all over with a little olive oil, and season with salt and pepper.
Pull the pan out. Push the broccoli to the edges and place the salmon fillets, evenly spaced, in the middle. Brush the salmon generously with the glaze.
Return to the oven and roast about 12 minutes, until the salmon is cooked through but still a touch translucent in the middle and the broccoli is browned at the tips.
Squeeze the lime over the broccoli, sprinkle the salmon with the reserved sliced scallions and sesame seeds, and serve.
Make ahead (Tue evening, ~10 min) Whisk the glaze and store in a small jar in the fridge—it keeps for a week. Cut broccoli into florets and store in a container with a damp paper towel. Grate ginger only the day-of; it oxidizes fast.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Pull a small piece of salmon aside before brushing it with the glaze (which is high-sodium). Roast their plain piece alongside, then flake it carefully, checking for pin bones. For the 13-month-old: cut broccoli florets small and serve well-cooked; stems are easier to chew than the tops. The 34-month-old usually loves a little sesame seed sprinkle and a drizzle of the glaze on the side, like a dip.
Friday · Japanese-inspired

Miso-Honey Chicken
with Asparagus

Adapted from Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking) by Yossy Arefi
25 mintotal
10 minactive
Broilersheet pan, ~6" from heat
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 3 Tbspwhite miso paste
  • 3 Tbspmild honey
  • 3 Tbspsoy sauce (pantry)
  • 1 Tbsprice vinegar
  • 2 tspfresh ginger, peeled & grated
  • 2 tspgarlic, grated
  • 2 tspchile-garlic sauce (or sriracha)
  • 1 Tbsp + 2 tspneutral oil (grapeseed/canola)
  • 1½–2 lbboneless, skinless chicken thighs
  • 1 lbasparagus, trimmed
  • 2scallions, thinly sliced
  • —kosher salt & black pepper
  • opt.steamed rice (pantry)

Method

Whisk together the miso, honey, soy sauce, rice vinegar, ginger, garlic, chile-garlic sauce, 1 Tbsp of the neutral oil, and 1 Tbsp water in a small bowl. Refrigerate half of this mixture—it becomes your finishing sauce.
Put the chicken in a bowl or zip-top bag, pour the remaining miso-honey over it, and toss to coat. Marinate in the fridge 15–30 minutes (no longer—it can dry the chicken).
Heat the broiler with a rack about 6" from the heat. Line a large sheet pan with foil.
Remove chicken from marinade, scrape off any excess that's clinging in thick patches. Lay flatter side up on one half of the pan in a single layer.
Put the asparagus on the other half. Drizzle with the remaining 2 tsp oil, salt, and pepper; toss and spread in one layer.
Broil about 10 minutes, until the chicken is charred in spots and cooked through (165°F) and the asparagus is browned at the tips. Watch closely—broilers vary; rotate the pan halfway if your broiler has hot spots.
Drizzle the chicken with the reserved miso-honey sauce, scatter scallions, and serve. Rice optional.
Make ahead (Thu evening, ~10 min) Whisk the full marinade and divide it now—keep the finishing half in a small jar. Trim the asparagus and store wrapped in a damp paper towel. Don't marinate the chicken yet; you only want 15–30 min of marinade time so do it Friday after work.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo The marinade is high-sodium. Set aside one small chicken thigh unmarinated; broil it plain alongside (it'll cook in the same time or slightly less—check at 8 min). Shred it small for the 13-month-old. The asparagus is fine for both kids: cut into ½" pieces for the toddler, finer for the baby. Honey is fine for both children (the AAP cutoff is 12 months, both are over that).

Working with the kids

A few across-the-board notes for the 13- and 34-month portions:

  • Salt: The 13-month-old's daily sodium ceiling is ~1 g. Wed's mushrooms (soy sauce), Thu's salmon glaze, and Fri's miso marinade are all high-sodium. Strategy: pull aside their portion before the salty element goes in.
  • Beef for the 13-mo: Thinly sliced and finely chopped—or skip and offer extra mushrooms, which they often prefer anyway. The AAP doesn't restrict beef but it's a chewy protein.
  • Choking hazards: Whole mushrooms cut to ¼" for the 13-mo. Broccoli florets cooked through and cut small. Asparagus cut into ½" pieces.
  • Honey: Both kids are over 12 months, so honey is fine.

Substitutions, if Fresh is missing something

  • Strip steak → rib-eye or sirloin (same method, watch the thermometer). Avoid tenderloin for this method—too lean, gets dry.
  • Cremini mushrooms → button mushrooms work but have less flavor. Avoid pre-sliced; they're already losing water.
  • Fresh thyme → ½ tsp dried thyme in the mushrooms. In the steak basting, dried isn't great—if you can't get fresh, skip it; a sprig of fresh rosemary works too.
  • White miso → 2 Tbsp tahini + 1 tsp extra soy sauce. Different but workable.
  • Salmon → trout, arctic char, or even cod (reduce roast time to ~9 min for cod).

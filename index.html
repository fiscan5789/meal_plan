Monday · Vietnamese-inspired

Pork Meatballs with
Ginger & Fish Sauce
Double batch · freeze half

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Kay Chun.
25 mintotal (doubled)
20 minactive
Oven 425°F+ skillet for greens
Eat 12, freeze 12uses 2 lb pork

Meatballs Doubled to 2 lb pork

  • 4 Tbsppeeled, minced fresh ginger
  • 2 Tbspminced garlic (about 6 large cloves)
  • 2 Tbspfish sauce
  • 2 tspblack pepper (pantry)
  • 1 tspkosher salt (pantry)
  • 1 cupfinely crushed Ritz crackers (24 crackers)
  • 2 lbground pork (your frozen pork, thawed)

Quick vegetable side

  • 12 ozsnap peas or green beans, trimmed
  • 1 Tbspneutral oil
  • 1 tsptoasted sesame oil (pantry)
  • —flaky salt

To serve

  • —steamed rice (pantry)
  • opt.fresh basil or cilantro, lettuce cups
  • opt. dip¼ cup mayo + 2 tsp sesame oil or soy sauce

Method

Heat the oven to 425°F. Line a rimmed baking sheet with parchment or grease it lightly. (If you'll have all 24 meatballs in the oven at once, use two sheets.)
In a large bowl, combine the ginger, garlic, fish sauce, pepper, salt, crushed Ritz, and pork. Mix gently with your hands — don't overwork.
Shape into 24 golf-ball-size meatballs (about 2"). Divide: 12 onto the lined baking sheet for tonight; the other 12 onto a separate parchment-lined plate or small tray for the freezer.
Freeze 12: place that tray in the freezer uncovered until the meatballs are solid (~2 hours), then transfer to a freezer bag. Label "Pork meatballs, raw, [date]". Keeps 2 months. (To cook later: bake from frozen at 425°F, ~20 min.)
Bake tonight's 12: until golden and cooked through, about 15 minutes.
While they bake, cook the side: heat 1 Tbsp neutral oil in a skillet over high. Add the snap peas (or green beans). Cook, tossing, 60 seconds for char, then 1–2 min more until crisp-tender. Off heat, toss with the toasted sesame oil and a pinch of flaky salt.
Serve meatballs with the vegetable and rice. Add lettuce cups, herbs, and the mayo dip if you want a fuller spread.
Why freeze raw Raw meatballs freeze better than cooked — less freezer-burn, no rubbery reheat. The cookbook itself recommends always making a multiple batch of these specifically to freeze. To use the frozen 12 later: bake straight from frozen at 425°F for ~20 min (5 min longer than fresh). No thawing needed. A future 20-minute dinner.
Make ahead (Sunday, ~15 min) — the only real prep this week Thaw 2 lb of the pork in the fridge overnight Saturday. Sunday: mince the ginger and garlic together; jar with the fish sauce. Crush 24 Ritz crackers. You can even mix and shape all 24 meatballs Sunday — refrigerate tonight's 12, freeze the other 12. Monday is then just bake + quick side.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo For the 13-mo: mix 2 Tbsp of the pork before adding fish sauce, salt, and pepper — just pork, ginger, garlic, crackers — and shape 2 mini meatballs. Or pinch apart baked ones; the seasoning is bold but fine in small amounts. Snap peas should be cut in thirds for the toddler, finely chopped for the baby (pods can be stringy).
Tuesday · Italian · Vegetarian

Pasta e Ceci
Italian pasta and chickpea stew

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Colu Henry.
30 mintotal
30 minactive (mostly simmering)
Stovetopone Dutch oven
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 3 Tbspolive oil, plus more for drizzling (pantry)
  • 1medium yellow onion, finely chopped
  • 3 clovesgarlic, finely chopped
  • 2 tspchopped fresh rosemary
  • ½ tspred pepper flakes (pantry)
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • 1 packed cupdrained canned whole tomatoes
  • 1 (15-oz) canchickpeas, drained & rinsed
  • 1 cupditalini (or other small pasta)
  • 4 cupsroughly chopped escarole, Tuscan kale, or radicchio
  • to servegrated pecorino or Parmesan, good olive oil

Method

Heat 3 Tbsp oil in a large Dutch oven or stockpot over medium. Add the onion and cook, stirring, until softened but not browned, 4–5 min. Add the garlic, rosemary, and red pepper flakes; cook 1 min more. Season well with salt and pepper.
Stir in the tomatoes and chickpeas, breaking up the tomatoes with the back of a spoon and smashing about ½ cup of the chickpeas (this gives the stew its creamy body).
Add 3 cups water, increase the heat to high, and bring to a boil. Add the pasta and simmer, stirring often so nothing sticks, until the pasta is al dente, about 10 min. The water will mostly be absorbed; for a brothier stew, add ½–1 cup more water and simmer 1 min more.
Add the escarole (or kale/radicchio) and stir until wilted. Taste and adjust salt and pepper.
Ladle into bowls; top with grated cheese and a drizzle of olive oil.
Make ahead (Sunday, 5 min) Chop the onion and store covered. Chop the garlic and rosemary together; store in a small jar. That's all that helps in advance — this is a same-night one-pot dish. (Don't pre-cook the pasta; it cooks in the stew.)
A note on leftovers Unlike the pasta e fagiole from a couple weeks ago, this dish cooks the pasta directly in the stew, so it doesn't freeze or keep as well — the pasta keeps absorbing liquid and softens. Best eaten the night it's made or within 2 days (add water when reheating). Make the full batch; it's a generous 4 servings plus toddler portions.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo A great kid dish — soft pasta, creamy chickpeas, mild. For the 13-mo: ladle a portion before adding red pepper flakes if your kid is sensitive (or just use a light hand — ½ tsp across the whole pot is mild). Mash a few chickpeas; cut the escarole small. For the 34-mo: usually an easy win, especially with extra cheese on top.
Thursday · Greek-inspired

Sheet-Pan Shrimp with
Tomatoes, Feta & Oregano

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Mark Bittman.
10 mintotal
10 minactive
Broilersheet pan
4 servingsuses your 1 lb shrimp

Ingredients

  • 1 clovegarlic
  • 1 tspkosher salt (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspchopped fresh oregano (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 1 Tbspfresh lemon juice
  • 2 Tbspolive oil (pantry)
  • —lots of black pepper (pantry)
  • 1 lbextra-large shrimp, peeled (your frozen shrimp, thawed)
  • —chopped tomatoes, for serving
  • —crumbled feta, for serving
  • to servecrusty bread and/or a hearty salad

Method

Heat the broiler with a rack close to the heat source.
In a small bowl, mash the garlic with the salt until a paste forms. Add the oregano, lemon juice, olive oil, and lots of black pepper; mix well. Rub the paste all over the shrimp.
Spread the shrimp in a single layer on a sheet pan. Broil 2–3 minutes per side, until pink and opaque. (Very large shrimp may need an extra minute — slice one to check; it should be opaque throughout.)
Serve topped with the chopped tomatoes and crumbled feta, with bread and/or salad alongside.
Make ahead (Wed evening, ~5 min) Thaw the shrimp: move from freezer to fridge Wednesday morning, or quick-thaw in a bowl of cold water (~15 min) Thursday. Make the garlic-oregano paste and store in a jar. Chop the tomatoes Thursday (they get watery if cut too early). Truly a 10-minute dinner once the shrimp is thawed.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Shrimp is fine developmentally for both kids; if either hasn't had shellfish, introduce small and watch for 2 hours. For the 13-mo: the broiled shrimp is well-seasoned but not spicy — chop a couple into small pieces; go light on the feta (salty). For the 34-mo: shrimp with a little tomato and feta is usually a hit. Serve with bread for dunking.

The iron strategy

4 of 5 meals are meaningfully iron-rich (you asked for 3). Mon and Thu use beef — the highest heme-iron source per ounce. Tue lentils provide ~6.5 mg per cooked cup of non-heme iron. Wed chicken thighs (dark meat) bring ~1.3 mg per thigh. Fri pairs spinach with shrimp, both contributors. Lemon, tomatoes, and yogurt appear throughout — vitamin C and animal protein both significantly boost non-heme iron absorption from lentils and spinach.

The Tuesday lentils need a head-start

Clark's recipe simmers brown lentils for 30–60 min. To stay inside your 30-min weeknight ceiling, we cook the lentils Sunday evening as part of the foundation prep and reheat them Tuesday in about 5 minutes. The yogurt sauce, shallots, and spice base get reheated and the eggs are poached fresh.

Monday · Levantine

Spicy Ground Beef Kebabs
with Tomato-Sumac Sauce

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights Mediterranean by Christopher Kimball (2021). Take on Levantine kebab hindi.
40 mintotal
20 minactive
Broilersheet pan
4–6 servings+ toddler portions

Kebabs

  • 1½ lb80% lean ground beef
  • ½large red onion, finely chopped
  • ¼ cupfinely chopped fresh cilantro stems
  • ¼ cuppine nuts, chopped
  • 1½ tspground cinnamon
  • 1½ tspground allspice
  • 4 tsphot paprika (or 3½ tsp sweet paprika + ½ tsp cayenne)
  • 2 tspkosher salt (pantry)
  • 1½ tspblack pepper (pantry)

Tomato-Sumac Sauce

  • ½large red onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 pintscherry or grape tomatoes
  • 2 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 1 Tbspground sumac
  • ½ tspkosher salt
  • ¼ tspblack pepper

To serve

  • ½ cupfresh cilantro leaves, roughly chopped
  • opt.rice or warm flatbread (pantry)

Method

Heat the broiler with a rack about 6" from the element. Line a rimmed baking sheet with foil.
In a large bowl, combine the beef, chopped red onion, cilantro stems, pine nuts, cinnamon, allspice, paprika, 2 tsp salt, and 1½ tsp pepper. Mix well with your hands.
Divide into 12 portions and shape each into a patty about 2½–3" across. Arrange in a single layer on the prepared baking sheet.
Broil until well browned on top and the centers hit 160°F, 7–9 minutes. Watch closely — broilers vary; rotate the pan halfway if your broiler has hot spots.
Using a wide metal spatula, transfer patties to a serving platter and cover loosely with foil. Discard any liquid pooled on the baking sheet, but keep the foil lining in place — you're using the same pan for the sauce.
In a medium bowl, toss the sliced red onion, tomatoes, olive oil, sumac, ½ tsp salt, and ¼ tsp pepper. Spread in a single layer on the same sheet pan.
Broil until the tomatoes burst and the onions char, 5–6 minutes.
Using the back of a fork, crush the tomatoes right on the pan to form a chunky sauce. Add 2 Tbsp water and stir gently. Taste and adjust salt.
Spoon the sauce over the patties, scatter cilantro leaves on top, and drizzle with additional olive oil. Serve with rice or flatbread if you'd like.
Why this hits iron hard Beef supplies heme iron, the form your body absorbs most readily — roughly 3 mg per 4-oz serving. The vitamin C in the broiled tomatoes further boosts iron uptake. Even a toddler portion of a few small kebab pieces meaningfully contributes to their daily iron needs (~7 mg/day for 1–3 year olds).
Make ahead (Sunday evening, ~20 min) Chop both halves of the red onion (one finely, one sliced) and store separately. Chop the cilantro stems. Mix the spice blend (cinnamon, allspice, paprika, salt, pepper) in a small jar. Monday after work, you'll just mix the meat, shape the patties, and broil.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo For the 13-mo: shape 2 mini patties from the seasoned mix (pinch them small), broil alongside, and break apart with a fork to serve — the cinnamon and allspice are mild for that age. Skip cayenne; if using the hot paprika, set aside their mini patties before adding it. For the 34-mo: half a patty is usually plenty. Both kids can have the tomato sauce (let it cool, mash for the 13-mo). Pine nuts are technically a choking risk under 4 — chopping them fine like the recipe specifies handles this for both ages.
Tuesday · Indian-inspired · Vegetarian

Curried Lentils with
Poached Eggs & Garlicky Yogurt

From Dinner by Melissa Clark (2017).
25 min day-of(lentils prepped Sunday)
15 minactive
Stovetoppot + skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Lentils & aromatics

  • 2 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • ½ cupdiced shallots (2–3 shallots)
  • 5 clovesgarlic (4 chopped, 1 reserved)
  • 2 Tbspgrated peeled fresh ginger
  • 1 Tbspgaram masala
  • 1½ tspground turmeric
  • 1½ tspground cumin
  • 12 ozbrown lentils (~1½ cups)
  • 1dried chile de arbol (or ¼ tsp red chile flakes)
  • 1 tspfine sea salt

Yogurt & eggs

  • ⅓ cupplain whole-milk Greek yogurt (thinned with 1 Tbsp milk)
  • 4–8large eggs (1–2 per adult)
  • —salt & pepper for the eggs

To serve

  • —fresh cilantro leaves
  • pinchred chile flakes
  • drizzlegood olive oil

Method — Tuesday night, lentils already cooked

Reheat the prepped lentils gently in a pot over medium-low, adding a splash of water if they've thickened. Taste, adjust salt. Cover and keep warm.
Make the yogurt sauce: in a small bowl, combine the yogurt with the milk to thin it. Grate the reserved garlic clove into the yogurt. Season with salt and pepper. Stir.
Poach the eggs: fill a wide skillet or pot with about 2" of salted water and bring to a bare simmer (small bubbles, not rolling). Crack one egg into a small cup. Slip it gently into the water in one fluid motion. Repeat with the remaining eggs (work in 2 batches if needed).
Simmer the eggs without disturbing them until the whites are set but yolks remain runny, 2–3 minutes. Use a slotted spoon to transfer eggs to a paper-towel-lined plate.
To serve: spoon hot lentils into shallow bowls. Place 1–2 eggs on top of each serving. Season the eggs with salt and pepper. Dollop with yogurt sauce, drizzle with olive oil, scatter cilantro leaves and a few chile flakes on top.
Why this hits iron hard Brown lentils are one of the most iron-dense foods: 6.6 mg of non-heme iron per cooked cup. Eggs add another ~1 mg each. Non-heme iron is less easily absorbed than the heme iron in beef, but the turmeric, ginger, and citric acid in the yogurt boost uptake substantially. A squeeze of lemon at the table helps further.
Make ahead (Sunday evening, ~50 min — mostly hands-off) Heat 2 Tbsp olive oil in a medium pot over medium. Add diced shallots and a pinch of salt; cook 5 min until softened. Add chopped garlic (4 cloves), ginger, garam masala, turmeric, and cumin; cook 1 min until fragrant. Stir in the lentils, add 4½ cups water, the dried chile, and 1 tsp salt. Bring to a boil, reduce to medium-low, simmer uncovered 30–45 min until tender but not mushy. Cool, refrigerate. Tuesday is just a reheat.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Lentils are excellent for both kids — soft, easy to chew, deeply nutritious. For the 13-mo: spoon out a small portion before seasoning is fully adjusted. Mash slightly with a fork. Top with a tiny dollop of plain (un-garlicked) yogurt. Skip the chile flakes. For the 34-mo: many toddlers love runny egg yolks on lentils — basically savory custard. If they're new to poached eggs, scramble theirs and let them dip toast in the lentils.
Wednesday · North African / Mediterranean

Harissa Chicken
with Leeks, Potatoes & Yogurt

From Dinner by Melissa Clark (2017).
1 hr 10 min+ 30 min marinade
15 minactive
Oven 425°Fsheet pan
3–4 servings+ toddler portions

Chicken & potatoes

  • 1½ lbbone-in, skin-on chicken thighs and drumsticks
  • 1¼ lbYukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cut into 1 x ½" chunks
  • 2½ tspkosher salt (pantry)
  • ½ tspfreshly ground black pepper (pantry)
  • 2 Tbspharissa paste
  • ½ tspground cumin
  • 4½ Tbspextra-virgin olive oil, divided (pantry)

Leeks & yogurt

  • 2leeks (white and light green only), halved lengthwise, rinsed, sliced into half-moons
  • ½ tspgrated lemon zest
  • ¼ tspkosher salt
  • ⅓ cupplain whole-milk yogurt (or Greek thinned with milk)
  • 1small garlic clove
  • ¼ tspkosher salt + ¼ tsp pepper for yogurt

To serve

  • 1 cupmixed soft fresh herbs (dill, parsley, mint, cilantro)
  • —fresh lemon juice
  • drizzleolive oil

Method

Combine the chicken and potatoes in a large bowl. Season with 2½ tsp salt and ½ tsp pepper. In a small bowl, whisk together the harissa, cumin, and 3 Tbsp olive oil. Pour over the chicken and potatoes and toss to coat.
Let stand at room temperature for 30 min while the oven preheats. (This marinade time matters for the spice to penetrate.)
Meanwhile, in a medium bowl, combine the sliced leeks, lemon zest, ¼ tsp salt, and the remaining 1½ Tbsp olive oil. Set aside.
Heat the oven to 425°F.
Arrange the chicken and potatoes in a single layer on a large rimmed baking sheet (don't crowd — use two pans if tight). Roast 20 min.
Pull the pan out. Toss the potatoes lightly with tongs. Scatter the leeks over the baking sheet. Return to the oven and roast 20–25 min more, until the chicken is cooked through (175°F for thighs & legs — dark meat can go higher than 165°F without drying), the potatoes are golden, and the leeks are wilted with crispy edges.
While the chicken cooks, prepare the yogurt: place yogurt in a small bowl, grate the garlic clove over it, season with ¼ tsp salt and ¼ tsp pepper. Stir.
Spoon the yogurt over the chicken and vegetables right on the baking sheet (or transfer to a platter if you want to be fancy). Scatter the herbs over the yogurt, drizzle with olive oil and a squeeze of lemon juice. Serve.
Why this hits iron hard Chicken thighs (dark meat) carry roughly twice the iron of breast meat — ~1.3 mg per thigh, all heme iron. Leeks contribute a small additional amount, and the lemon and herbs increase absorption. Less iron-dense than the beef nights, but a solid contributor.
Make ahead (Tuesday evening, ~15 min) Peel and chunk the potatoes — store covered in cold water in the fridge to prevent browning, drain and pat dry Wednesday before tossing with the marinade. Halve, rinse, and slice the leeks; store wrapped in damp paper towel in a container. Mix the harissa-cumin-oil marinade in a small jar. Wash and dry the herbs, wrap in paper towel inside a bag.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Harissa varies a lot in heat — taste yours before deciding. For the 13-mo: set aside a few potato chunks and a piece of chicken before the harissa marinade, season with just salt & oil, and roast in the corner of the same pan. Shred the cooked chicken; mash a potato chunk. Plain (un-garlicked) yogurt on the side. For the 34-mo: a mild harissa is usually fine — offer their chicken with a small dollop of yogurt to cool it. Leeks are sweet when roasted; most toddlers like them.
Thursday · Chinese-inspired

Black Pepper Beef
& Cabbage Stir-Fry

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Sue Li.
22 mintotal
22 minactive
Stovetopcast iron or wok
4 servings+ toddler portions (scaled up)

Ingredients Scaled to 1¼ lb beef

  • 1½ Tbspblack peppercorns, coarsely crushed
  • 5 clovesgarlic, grated
  • 1 Tbsplight brown sugar
  • 1½ tspcornstarch
  • 1½ tspkosher salt (pantry)
  • 1¼ lbsirloin steak, thinly sliced crosswise (against the grain)
  • 3 Tbspsunflower or other neutral oil
  • 3 Tbspsoy sauce (pantry)
  • ½ headsmall green cabbage (~8 oz), thinly sliced
  • 1 Tbspsherry vinegar
  • 1 Tbsptoasted sesame seeds, crushed lightly
  • 2scallions, thinly sliced
  • —hot cooked rice, for serving (pantry)

Method

Crush the peppercorns: put them in a zip-top bag and smash with the bottom of a heavy skillet or rolling pin. You want coarse pieces, not powder.
In a medium bowl, stir together the peppercorns, grated garlic, brown sugar, cornstarch, and 1½ tsp salt. Add the sliced steak and toss to coat. (You can marinate up to 8 hours ahead.)
Heat 2 Tbsp of the oil in a large cast iron skillet (or wok) over medium-high. When it shimmers, add half the steak in a single layer — 1¼ lb won't sear properly all at once; crowded beef steams.
Cook the first batch, stirring frequently, until the edges are lightly browned, about 3 minutes. Add 1½ Tbsp of the soy sauce and toss to coat, 30 seconds. Transfer to a bowl with a slotted spoon.
Add the remaining 1 Tbsp oil to the pan, heat briefly, then cook the second half of the steak the same way: 3 minutes, then 1½ Tbsp soy sauce, then transfer to the bowl with the first batch.
With the skillet still over medium-high, add the sliced cabbage and spread in an even layer. Let cook undisturbed for 1 minute so some pieces caramelize.
Toss the cabbage and cook, stirring occasionally, until crisp-tender, 4–6 minutes. Stir in the sherry vinegar and season with salt.
Return all the steak and any juices to the skillet. Stir until well combined with the cabbage and warmed through, about 1 minute.
Top with the crushed sesame seeds and scallions. Serve with hot rice.
Why this hits iron hard Sirloin contributes about 2.5–3 mg of heme iron per serving. Cabbage is rich in vitamin C, which boosts iron absorption from the meal as a whole. A fast weeknight way to get a meaningful iron dose.
Make ahead (Wednesday evening, ~10 min) Slice the 1¼ lb sirloin thin against the grain (chill in the freezer 15 min first — makes slicing much easier). Crush the peppercorns. Mix the dry rub (peppercorns, garlic, brown sugar, cornstarch, salt) and toss with the beef — can marinate up to 8 hours. Thinly slice the cabbage and store in a container. Slice scallions and store separately.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo The black pepper is a lot. For both kids, pull out a portion of the beef before tossing with the peppercorn rub, and quickly stir-fry it plain with a little cabbage and a few drops of soy sauce. For the 13-mo: chop the beef very fine, almost shredded. For the 34-mo: dice into small pieces. Cabbage is fine for both ages cooked tender.
Friday · Greek

Greek-Style Spinach Rice
with Shrimp & Dill

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights Mediterranean by Christopher Kimball (2021). Spanakorizo with shrimp.
45 mintotal
15 minactive
Stovetopone large saucepan
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 2 Tbspsalted butter
  • 10 ozbaby spinach (two 5-oz containers)
  • 2 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 2medium shallots, halved and thinly sliced
  • 1½ cupslong-grain white rice, rinsed and drained
  • 1 lbextra-large shrimp (21/25 per lb), peeled, deveined, tails removed, patted dry
  • 1 cuplightly packed fresh dill, roughly chopped
  • 1 tspgrated lemon zest + 3 Tbsp lemon juice
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • opt.crumbled feta cheese, to serve

Method

Important: use a saucepan, not a wide Dutch oven. A narrower diameter is essential here — a wide pot causes uneven rice and the shrimp won't steam properly.
In a large saucepan over medium-high heat, melt the butter until bubbling. Add the spinach and cook, stirring, until wilted but still bright green, about 1 minute. Transfer to a bowl including the liquid it released — you'll add that back at the end.
Set the same pan over medium-high. Add the olive oil, shallots, and ½ tsp salt. Cook, stirring often, until the shallots are translucent, about 2 minutes.
Add the rinsed rice, 2½ cups water, ½ tsp salt, and ¼ tsp pepper. Stir to combine and bring to a boil.
Cover, reduce heat to low, and cook until the liquid has been absorbed, 15–18 minutes. Don't peek — you need to trap that heat. Meanwhile, season the shrimp with salt and pepper.
When the rice is done, remove the pan from the heat. Quickly and evenly scatter the shrimp over the surface, then re-cover immediately. Let stand 10 minutes — the residual heat cooks the shrimp through.
Using a fork, fluff the rice and fold in the shrimp. Re-cover and let stand 5–7 more minutes until the shrimp are fully opaque throughout.
Add the wilted spinach with its liquid, the dill, lemon zest and juice. Fold until well combined. Taste and adjust salt and pepper.
Serve drizzled with additional olive oil and a sprinkle of feta if you'd like.
Why this hits iron hard Cooked spinach concentrates iron — 10 oz fresh reduces to a substantial pile of cooked greens with about 6–7 mg total iron, plus 2–3 mg from the shrimp. The lemon juice (vitamin C) at the end multiplies the absorption of the non-heme iron from the spinach. Classic Greek pairing, nutrition-by-accident.
Make ahead (Thursday evening, ~10 min) Halve and slice the shallots. Rinse and drain the rice; store in a covered container. Chop the dill. Zest the lemon, juice it, store separately. If you bought shell-on shrimp, peel and devein them tonight; pat very dry and store on a paper-towel-lined plate, covered.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Shrimp is fine developmentally for both kids; check with your pediatrician on shellfish allergies if either has any concerning food history. For the 13-mo: chop a few shrimp into very small pieces or mash with rice. For the 34-mo: shrimp halved or quartered, mixed into rice. Both kids will love the lemon-dill flavor. Watch the feta if using — it's salty; skip for the 13-mo, small amount fine for the 34-mo.

The iron picture, summed up

Adult women need 18 mg of iron/day, men need 8 mg. This week roughly delivers:

  • Mon (beef kebabs): ~3.5 mg/serving, heme
  • Tue (lentils + egg): ~7 mg/serving, mostly non-heme
  • Wed (chicken thighs): ~1.5 mg/serving, heme
  • Thu (beef stir-fry): ~3 mg/serving, heme
  • Fri (spinach + shrimp): ~4 mg/serving, mixed

Approximate per cookbook-portion estimates and USDA data. Lemon, tomatoes, and vitamin-C-rich vegetables in most meals significantly boost non-heme iron absorption.

Working with the kids

  • Iron needs: 1–3 year olds need 7 mg/day. The lentil and beef nights are especially good for them.
  • Salt & spice: Pull aside their portion before bold seasonings. Pattern repeats Mon (cayenne), Tue (chile), Wed (harissa), Thu (peppercorns).
  • Pine nuts (Mon): Recipe specifies chopped, which makes them safe for both kids.
  • Shrimp allergy: If neither kid has had shellfish, introduce small. Watch for 2 hours. Check with your pediatrician on first introductions.
  • Eggs (Tue): Both kids can have soft-poached eggs. Cook longer if you prefer the yolk less runny.

Substitutions, if Fresh is missing something

  • Harissa: Mix 1 Tbsp tomato paste + 1 tsp smoked paprika + ½ tsp cayenne + ½ tsp cumin + 1 grated garlic clove + 1 Tbsp olive oil per 2 Tbsp.
  • Sumac: Lemon zest + a pinch of salt.
  • Pine nuts: Slivered almonds or chopped walnuts.
  • Sherry vinegar: Red wine vinegar + pinch of sugar. Or rice vinegar.
  • Brown lentils: Green lentils work identically. Avoid red — they break down.
  • Sirloin (Thu): Skirt, flank, or hanger steak. Avoid filet — too lean.

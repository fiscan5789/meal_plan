Pregnancy-safe, by design

Congratulations. Every meal this week is built to the standard pregnancy food-safety guidelines: all meat and poultry cooked fully through (no pink), salmon cooked to 145°F (not the medium-rare the original recipe calls for), no runny eggs, no alcohol, and only pasteurized/cooked cheeses. Where it fits naturally, the meals lean iron-rich (beef, beans) and folate-rich (beans, leafy greens, peas) — both especially valuable now. Specific notes are on each recipe's Pregnancy tag.

Your specific requests, mapped

Red onion early: Monday's keema matar (1 large red onion). Yellow onion early: Tuesday's musakhan (1 large yellow onion). Canned diced tomatoes: Monday and Friday. Canned black beans: Wednesday (2 cans, the star) and Friday (1 can, with the picadillo).

One feta note

No feta this week — but a general heads-up for future weeks: soft cheeses like feta are fine during pregnancy only if pasteurized (most US supermarket feta is — check the label) and safest when cooked into a dish. I'll keep flagging this whenever a recipe calls for it.

Monday · Indian

Indian-Spiced Beef & Peas
(Keema Matar)

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights by Christopher Kimball (2018).
40 mintotal
25 minactive
Stovetop12" skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 14½-oz candiced tomatoes (your canned diced tomatoes)
  • 2 Tbsptomato paste
  • 3 Tbspgrapeseed or other neutral oil
  • 1 largered onion, finely chopped (your red onion)
  • 4 clovesgarlic, finely grated
  • 4 tspfinely grated fresh ginger
  • 3 Tbspgaram masala
  • 1 lb90% lean ground beef
  • 2 cupsfrozen peas
  • ¼ cupplain whole-milk yogurt, plus more to serve
  • ⅓ cupchopped fresh cilantro
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • to servebasmati rice or warm naan (pantry)

Method

In a medium bowl, stir the diced tomatoes (with their juices), ½ cup water, and the tomato paste together. Set aside.
In a 12" skillet over medium-high, heat the oil until shimmering. Measure out and reserve ¼ cup of the red onion (for garnish). Add the rest to the skillet and cook, stirring, until softened and beginning to brown, about 6 min.
Add the garlic and ginger; cook, stirring constantly, until fragrant, about 1 min. Reduce to low, add the garam masala and ½ tsp pepper, and cook, stirring, until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
Stir in the tomato mixture. Bring to a simmer over medium and cook, stirring and scraping up spices, until slightly thickened, about 5 min.
Break the ground beef into 1–2" pieces and add on top of the tomato mixture — don't stir. Sprinkle with salt (start with 1 tsp; adjust later), cover, reduce to medium-low, and cook without stirring until the beef is no longer pink on the exterior, about 6 min.
Stir in the peas and cook, uncovered, breaking up the beef, until the meat is fully cooked through with no pink remaining, another 4–5 min. Pregnancy: make sure all the beef has cooked past pink — ground beef should reach 160°F.
Remove from heat and let stand, covered, 5 min. Stir in the yogurt. Serve topped with cilantro, the reserved chopped red onion, and additional yogurt, with rice or naan.
Pregnancy Cook the ground beef fully — no pink, 160°F. The recipe's natural endpoint already gets you there; just don't pull it early. Yogurt is pasteurized (standard for US supermarket yogurt) and stirred in off-heat, which is fine. This is one of the most iron-rich meals of the week: beef plus peas, with the tomatoes' vitamin C boosting absorption.
Make ahead (Sunday, ~10 min) Finely chop the red onion (keep the ¼ cup garnish portion separate). Grate the garlic and ginger together; jar them. Mix the tomato-paste-and-diced-tomato base in a covered container. Monday is then a 25-min cook.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Garam masala is warming, not hot — usually fine for both kids, but taste first. For the 13-mo: spoon a small portion before the final salting; mash the peas slightly; a dollop of plain yogurt cools it. The beef is soft and well-broken-down. For the 34-mo: serve over rice; most toddlers like the mild curry flavor and the sweetness of the peas.
Tuesday · Palestinian

Sumac-Spiced Chicken
(Musakhan)

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights by Christopher Kimball (2018).
40 mintotal
25 minactive
Stovetop + ovenDutch oven
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 2 lbboneless, skinless chicken thighs, trimmed, cut into 2" pieces, patted dry
  • 6 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil, divided (pantry)
  • 1 largeyellow onion, halved & thinly sliced (your white/yellow onion)
  • 4 clovesgarlic, thinly sliced
  • ½ cuptoasted pine nuts (¼ cup chopped, ¼ cup whole)
  • 4 Tbspground sumac, divided
  • 2 tspsweet paprika
  • 48" pita bread rounds
  • ½ cupfresh flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • to servetahini, for drizzling
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)

Method

Heat the oven to 250°F (just for warming pita). Season the chicken with salt and pepper.
In a large Dutch oven over high, heat 1 Tbsp oil until barely smoking. Add half the chicken in a single layer; cook undisturbed until well browned, about 5 min. Transfer to a bowl. Repeat with 1 Tbsp oil and the rest of the chicken.
Add 2 Tbsp oil to the pot, heat over medium. Add the sliced yellow onion; cook, stirring, until softened, about 5 min. Add garlic, chopped pine nuts, 3 Tbsp of the sumac, and paprika; cook, stirring, until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
Stir in 3 cups water and bring to a simmer. Return the chicken and accumulated juices to the pot. Cover, reduce to medium-low, and cook until a skewer meets no resistance and the chicken is fully cooked through, about 10 min. Pregnancy: chicken should reach 165°F — cook until no pink remains.
Wrap the pita in foil and warm in the oven while the chicken simmers.
Transfer the chicken to a bowl. Bring the liquid to a simmer over medium-high and cook, stirring, until most of the moisture evaporates, 10–15 min. Shred the chicken with 2 forks.
Return the chicken to the pot, stir in the ¼ cup whole pine nuts. Taste and season. Brush the warm pita with the remaining 2 Tbsp oil, sprinkle with the last 1 Tbsp sumac, and cut in half.
Stir the parsley into the chicken. Serve with the pita and tahini for drizzling.
Pregnancy Cook the chicken fully through to 165°F (the braise step handles this). All ingredients here are pregnancy-safe. Sumac is a tart spice, not an herbal supplement — no concern. A solid iron source from the dark-meat thighs.
Make ahead (Monday evening, ~10 min) Slice the yellow onion and garlic; store covered. Toast and chop the pine nuts (keep chopped and whole separate). Cut the chicken into 2" pieces; refrigerate covered. Mix the 3 Tbsp + 1 Tbsp sumac portions into two labeled jars so you're not measuring mid-cook.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Shredded sumac chicken is tender and mild (tart, not spicy). For the 13-mo: chop the shredded chicken small; pine nuts are a choking risk, so use only the finely-chopped ones for their portion, not whole. Soft pita torn into small pieces works well. For the 34-mo: a little tahini drizzle is usually a hit; offer pita "dippers."

Cheesy, Spicy
Black Bean Bake

From Easy Weeknight Dinners (NYT Cooking, 2024) by Ali Slagle.
15 mintotal
10 minactive
Oven 475°Foven-safe skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 3 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 5 clovesgarlic, sliced
  • ¼ cuptomato paste
  • 1½ tspsmoked paprika
  • 1 tspground cumin
  • ¼ tspred pepper flakes (pantry)
  • 2 (14-oz) cansblack beans, drained & rinsed (your canned black beans)
  • ½ cupboiling water
  • 1½ cupsgrated sharp Cheddar or Manchego (6 oz)
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • to servewarm tortillas, rice, or a green salad

Method

Heat the oven to 475°F. In a 10" oven-safe skillet, heat the olive oil over medium-high. Fry the sliced garlic until lightly golden, about 1 min.
Stir in the tomato paste, smoked paprika, cumin, and red pepper flakes (careful of splattering). Fry 30 seconds, lowering the heat as needed so the garlic doesn't burn.
Add the black beans, boiling water, and generous pinches of salt and pepper. Stir to combine.
Sprinkle the cheese evenly over the top. Bake until the cheese has melted, 5–10 min. For more browning, run under the broiler 1–2 min.
Serve immediately with tortillas, rice, or a green salad.
Pregnancy A standout meal for now: black beans are rich in both folate and iron, two of the most important nutrients in pregnancy. The cheese is cooked/melted (always safe). Fully vegetarian and quick — a good one for a tired evening. Add a handful of baby spinach stirred in at the end for even more folate if you like.
Make ahead (almost none needed) This is a 15-minute dish start to finish, so there's little point prepping ahead. If you want: slice the garlic and grate the cheese the night before.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo The recipe author notes black beans are a huge kid hit. For the 13-mo: this version has red pepper flakes — scoop a small portion of beans before the spices if your child is sensitive (or just use a light hand). Mash the beans slightly; whole beans are a minor choking risk under 1, fine to mash. For the 34-mo: serve with a tortilla to scoop. The melted cheese is a reliable win.
Thursday · Vietnamese-inspired

Glazed Salmon
with Chili-Basil Sauce

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights by Christopher Kimball (2018).
40 mintotal
20 minactive
Stovetop + oven12" oven-safe skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Ingredients

  • 2 Tbspchili-garlic sauce
  • 2 Tbsppacked dark brown sugar
  • 1 Tbspfish sauce
  • 2 tspsoy sauce (pantry)
  • 2 tspcornstarch
  • 3 Tbspgrapeseed or other neutral oil, divided
  • 1 Tbspfinely grated fresh ginger
  • 1 stalklemon grass, trimmed to lower 6", minced (or 1 tsp extra ginger + lime zest)
  • 2 Tbsplemon juice
  • ½ cup + 1 Tbspchopped fresh basil (½ cup chopped, 1 Tbsp julienned)
  • 4 (6 oz)center-cut salmon fillets (1–1¼" thick), patted dry
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)
  • to serverice and steamed or stir-fried greens

Method

Heat the oven to 400°F with a rack in the middle. In a small bowl, stir ⅔ cup water, chili-garlic sauce, brown sugar, fish sauce, and soy sauce until the sugar dissolves. In another bowl, stir the cornstarch with 1 Tbsp water.
In a medium saucepan over medium-high, heat 2 Tbsp oil until shimmering. Add the ginger and lemon grass; cook until fragrant, about 10 seconds. Pour in the chili-garlic mixture and bring to a boil. Stir the cornstarch slurry to recombine, add it, reduce to medium-low, and simmer, stirring, until thickened, about 3 min. Off heat, stir in the lemon juice and ½ cup basil. Cover to keep warm.
Season the salmon on both sides with salt and pepper. In a 12" oven-safe skillet over medium-high, heat the remaining 1 Tbsp oil until barely smoking. Add the fillets flesh side down, reduce to medium, and cook until golden brown, about 4 min.
Transfer the skillet to the oven and cook until the salmon is fully opaque and flakes easily, 145°F at the thickest part — about 8–10 min. (This is longer than the original recipe's medium doneness — for pregnancy, cook the salmon all the way through.)
Peel off and discard the skin. Transfer fillets browned-side-up to a platter. Spoon half the sauce over the fish, sprinkle with the julienned basil, and serve the rest of the sauce on the side, with rice and greens.
Pregnancy Salmon is one of the best fish choices during pregnancy — low in mercury, high in DHA omega-3s, which support fetal brain development. The key change: cook it to 145°F (fully opaque), not the medium the recipe originally targets. The fish sauce and chili-garlic sauce are both fully cooked in the sauce. No alcohol anywhere in this dish.
Make ahead (Wednesday evening, ~10 min) Make the entire chili-basil sauce and refrigerate (add the basil only when reheating so it stays green). Grate the ginger and mince the lemon grass. Thursday is then just searing and roasting the salmon — about 15 min.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo The sauce has chili-garlic sauce (spicy) — serve the kids' salmon plain, sauce on the side or skipped. Pull a portion of salmon before saucing, flake carefully checking for pin bones. Salmon is excellent for both kids (omega-3s matter for them too). For the 34-mo, a tiny bit of the sauce as a dip is usually tolerated; taste-test first.

Cuban Picadillo
with Black Beans

From Milk Street: Tuesday Nights by Christopher Kimball (2018), with canned diced tomatoes and a black-bean side.
45 mintotal
30 minactive
Stovetop12" skillet
4 servings+ toddler portions

Picadillo

  • 2 Tbspextra-virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 2 mediumyellow onions, finely chopped
  • 6 clovesgarlic, minced
  • 1½ lb90% lean ground beef
  • ½ cupraisins, chopped
  • 1 Tbspcumin seeds (or 2 tsp ground cumin)
  • 1 tspdried oregano
  • 2 Tbsptomato paste
  • 1 (14½-oz) candiced tomatoes, drained (your canned diced tomatoes)
  • ½ cuppimiento-stuffed green olives, chopped, + 3 Tbsp brine
  • ½ cupminced fresh cilantro
  • —kosher salt & black pepper (pantry)

Quick black beans (side)

  • 1 (15-oz) canblack beans, with liquid (your canned black beans)
  • 1 Tbspolive oil (pantry)
  • 1 clovegarlic, minced
  • ½ tspground cumin
  • —salt to taste

To serve

  • —white rice (pantry)

Method

In a 12" nonstick skillet over medium-high, heat 2 Tbsp oil until shimmering. Add the chopped yellow onions and ½ tsp salt. Cook, stirring, until beginning to brown, about 5 min. Add the garlic; cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
Add the beef and cook, breaking it up, until no pink remains, 5–7 min. Pregnancy: cook fully to 160°F, no pink.
Stir in the raisins, cumin, oregano, tomato paste, and 1½ tsp salt; cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Stir in the drained diced tomatoes and ¾ cup water; bring to a simmer.
Reduce to medium and cook, stirring occasionally, until most of the liquid has evaporated, 7–9 min.
Stir in the olives; cook 1 min. Off heat, stir in the brine and cilantro. Taste and season.
Meanwhile, the black beans: in a small saucepan, warm 1 Tbsp oil over medium, add the garlic and cumin, cook 30 seconds, then add the beans with their liquid. Simmer 5–7 min until slightly thickened; season with salt.
Serve the picadillo over rice with the black beans alongside.
Pregnancy Iron and folate together: beef plus black beans. Cook the beef fully (160°F). The original recipe suggests a chopped hard-cooked egg garnish — that's fine in pregnancy as long as the egg is fully hard-cooked (no soft yolk), but I've left it off to keep things simple. No alcohol. The olives add sodium, so go easy on added salt.
Make ahead (Thursday evening, ~10 min) Chop both yellow onions; mince the garlic; chop the raisins and olives. Store covered. Friday is then a 30-min cook. The black bean side is fast enough to do same-night.
For the 13-mo & 34-mo Picadillo is sweet-savory and not spicy — usually a kid hit. The olives and brine make it salty, though: for the 13-mo, spoon out a portion before adding the olives and brine. Chop the raisins small (whole raisins are a choking risk under 4). The black beans are great for both kids — mash for the 13-mo.

Pregnancy food-safety, the working rules

  • Meat & poultry: fully cooked — chicken 165°F, ground beef 160°F, no pink.
  • Fish: cooked to 145°F; low-mercury only (salmon, shrimp, cod, trout, tilapia yes; swordfish, king mackerel, marlin, bigeye tuna no).
  • Eggs: fully cooked, no runny yolk.
  • Cheese: hard/cooked cheeses always fine; soft cheeses (feta, brie, queso fresco) only if pasteurized & ideally cooked.
  • Deli meat: only if heated to steaming.
  • No alcohol in cooking unless fully cooked off (I just avoid it).
  • Wash produce well; avoid raw sprouts.

General guidance, not medical advice — defer to your OB/midwife.

Iron & folate, where they are this week

  • Iron: beef (Mon, Fri), black beans (Wed, Fri), peas (Mon), dark-meat chicken (Tue).
  • Folate: black beans (Wed, Fri), peas (Mon), cilantro & parsley, any leafy greens you add.
  • Absorption tip: the vitamin C in the tomatoes, citrus, and peppers across these meals significantly boosts how much iron your body takes up from the beans and beef. The pairing is built in.
  • DHA: salmon (Thu).

Working with the kids

  • Mon (keema): mild curry, usually fine; mash peas, cool with yogurt for the 13-mo.
  • Tue (sumac chicken): tender & mild; use only finely-chopped pine nuts for the 13-mo (whole are a choking risk).
  • Wed (black bean bake): a known kid hit; mash beans, watch the pepper flakes for the 13-mo.
  • Thu (salmon): serve their portion plain (sauce is spicy); check for pin bones.
  • Fri (picadillo): reserve a portion before the salty olives/brine; chop raisins small.

Substitutions, if Fresh is missing something

  • Lemon grass (Thu): 1 extra tsp grated ginger + a strip of lime zest.
  • Pine nuts (Tue): the book is firm that walnuts/almonds aren't as good, but they work in a pinch; toast them first.
  • Manchego (Wed): sharp Cheddar (the recipe's first option).
  • Green olives (Fri): any briny green olive; just chop and reserve some brine.
  • Garam masala (Mon): no clean single sub; worth buying. Curry powder is different but works in a pinch.
